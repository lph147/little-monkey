# Little Monkey (小猴子): project notes for Claude Code

## About this project
A retro, Pokémon-style web game for learning Mandarin Chinese, themed on *Journey to the West*. The player is a baby monkey (Sun Wukong) who collects Chinese words like creatures, hears them again and again in simple sentences from the story's characters, and earns progress by practising. It's a vocabulary game: it never tests grammar.

- **DESIGN.md** is the plan and the source of truth for design decisions. Follow it. If a request conflicts with it, point that out before building.
- **The game's scope** (decided 8 November 2026): the novel's chapters 1 to 7, in five areas, ending where chapter 7 does, with Sun Wukong under the Five Elements Mountain. Areas 3 to 5 (The Eastern Sea, Heaven, Havoc in Heaven) are planned in DESIGN.md, with the owner's decisions; nothing is built yet, and each gets its own plan with mock-ups drawn by the game before it's built. The journey west (chapters 8 to 100) would be a second game.
- **Before Area 3** (decided 8 November 2026): Areas 1 and 2 are polished first, in DESIGN.md's Polishing Areas 1 and 2: a stinky snack, big and small creatures, day and night animals, new night animals, the old monkey's scroll, and last the gentler prologue (all six built and put live together, in one batch, at the owner's request). The prologue's own plan, beat by beat, was agreed with the owner the same day, as it's the first thing players see (the cave keeps its bed, table and bowls, truer to the story); its new moments are drawn by the game before it's built.
- **index.html** is the game. It loads `css/game.css`, then the content in `content/`, then the engine in `js/`, in the order listed in index.html; each script can use what the ones before it define. Until they have set the game up, a start-up screen covers the page (the app's monkey and three dots: `loading` on `<html>`, taken off at the end of js/main.js), so the console never shows half-built while the code downloads. All pixel art and sound are generated in code.

## Learning design rules (decided through playtesting; keep these)
- **One new word at a time.** Each new word has its own moment, its own card, and no line or step teaches two at once. Outside school, someone can go on from one new word to the next in the same conversation (decided 7 November 2026, after playing it: the quick word game that kept them apart made the story too slow, and was out of place); at school, the teacher still waits till you've practised (the area's `wordGap`), and each lesson teaches one. The exceptions are the school's first lessons (decided 24 October 2026): the first lesson of each kind teaches three words, one after another, each with its own card and questions before the next, then a mix (四 五 六, then 红色 黄色 白色), so two lessons open up chapter 1. Every lesson after that teaches one. And counting the troop into the cave (decided 3 November 2026: a quick word game before each monkey felt like an interruption every time): 一, 二 and 三 one after another, a monkey and a card each, then one matching game on the three.
- **New-word cards** show the picture, characters, pinyin, sound and English. No message after one says it's gone into your Word Scroll (decided 8 November 2026, the gentler prologue), and no glow either (the MENU button glowed instead till 9 November 2026, when it went after playing it: it led nowhere): the word is new in your scroll till you've seen the page it's on (`S.scrollNew`, the newest last, `markNew` in js/state.js; save version 40), the menu's 词 tile says how many words are new (just the number on the smallest phones), and the Word Scroll opens on the page with the newest, each new word tagged and a gold dot on each other tab that has some (`scrollNew` and `seenOn` in js/scrolls.js); the words the old monkey's scroll card shows coming back are seen. English is shown by default on the first card only; after that it is behind an English button. A card in a conversation (a character teaching the word, a reply, the shop) doesn't say the word again, so the conversation flows; 🔊 Hear it is there. A word you discover yourself (looking, stepping on something, picking it, meeting an animal) is said straight away.
- **Sentences:** pinyin over every word (a global setting can turn it off), never smaller than 10.5px, so its tone marks can be read on a small phone. Words the player has learned are green. The word being taught right now is bold green. Everything else is grey and "just for listening". Tapping any word shows its pinyin and meaning.
- **A sentence's English** is shown underneath the first three times you hear it, and again whenever you last heard it on an earlier day; otherwise it is behind an English button (`heardLine` in js/state.js; decided 5 November 2026, after playing it: shown only the very first time, lines heard ages ago came with no English).
- **Never test grammar.** A task involving a sentence always shows the whole sentence in Chinese (with pinyin) and English, with one word missing, and you choose the missing word. Never ask players to put words in order. The choices follow the word's level: its picture with the Chinese while it's new (Caught), Chinese and pinyin once recognised. A wrong choice shows the right one and play carries on (except in the shop, decided 29 October 2026: ask for something you didn't point at, and the 老板 is puzzled, a ? over him, and asks again, the same choices in another order, till you ask for it; only then do you pay, and asking again counts for nothing: the shop's `puzzled`, `buy()` in js/world.js).
- **Voice speed** (decided 5 November 2026, after a beginner playtested it): Settings' Voice speed, 100%, 90%, 80% or 70%, slows every piece of Chinese the game says, each clip stretched so the voice keeps its pitch (`stretchSamples` and `slowedClip` in js/sound.js; the device's own voice at that rate too); kept with each game (`S.voiceSpeed`). A 🐢 beside every 🔊 (any button whose text is just 🔊 and its words: `withTurtles` in js/art.js) says it once more slowly: two steps slower than your speed, never below 70%, and none at 70% (`turtleSpeed`).
- **Reply choices** show Chinese, pinyin and English every time, so replying is input, not a test, under what you're answering: the line just said (or what's happening, with a picture, such as a gift), put in plain English with what to do ("The hungry monkey thanks you. What do you say back?"). Settings, English on replies, turns the English off for players who know some Chinese. Any choice can be made; one that doesn't fit is shown with why (each phrase's `use`, in words.js) and the one that does, which your monkey then says, so the conversation makes sense.
- **In English UI text** (tasks, messages, notes), a word you know can be written as 汉字 (English), e.g. "Give the 水 (water) to the thirsty monkey", but only two at most in a sentence (best one: the word the sentence is about), never two side by side, and never a describing word on a word ("a red 花 (flower)", not "a 红色 (red) 花 (flower)", which reads as Chinese without its 的). Everything else stays plain English, so a sentence never looks like Chinese in English word order. Tasks for words not yet learned are written in plain English.
- **What do you say?** (decided 4 November 2026): phrases are practised by questions too, not only by replying: a moment in plain English (each phrase's `situation` in words.js), three phrases you know in Chinese and pinyin, their English once you've answered (`phraseQ` in js/quiz.js): near the end of a monkey's word game (never an animal's), and the third of every six in quick practice, once you know three. 对不起 and 没关系 are taught in chapter 1 (the chaser bumping into you on the hillside, your tail knocking over the stone tower), not by the hungry monkey; an event that teaches a word could wait as a talk part does, `wordReady` (none does now: decided 7 November 2026).
- **Words that sound alike are shown side by side** (decided 30 October 2026): the first time you catch a word spelt the same in pinyin as one you know (喝 hē after 河 hé, 鹿 lù after 路 lù), Sounds like ▸ after its card (and How it's made) shows both, their tones drawn and named, with Hear both (`soundsCard` in js/cards.js, `soundsLike` finding the pair as `toneless` does for listening questions); seen again, from the Word Scroll, after How it's made.
- **二 and 两** (decided 9 November 2026, explained properly so each can be used where it's right): 二 (èr) is for counting, 一，二，三 (counting aloud as you pick things up or do them one after another, hide and seek, the race's 一，二，三，跑！, the troop counted into the cave); 两 (liǎng) for two of something, how many there are (number questions, counting games and the How many card, `HOW_MANY_WORDS` and `howManyOf` in js/world.js; `COUNTING` keeps 二), so 二 is never an answer to how many, and two things to count only come once you know 两. 两 is the second maths lesson at school, after 四 五 六: the teacher counts two peaches onto his table with 二, then says how many there are with 两 (一，二。两个桃子！), and its card goes on to 二 or 两? (`SIDE_BY_SIDE`): 二 for counting (一，二，三！), 两 for two of something (两个桃子, 两只猴子), Counting? 二. How many? 两. A game that doesn't know 两 yet (one from before it was a lesson) learns it where it first meets it: the How many card (`knowTwo` in js/world.js, `TWO_NOTE`), the woodcutter's two stones (his fetch's `teach`), or a stall's price, two coins, 两文钱 (`TWO_WEN` in the human world's file: the town's fruit seller, and the 老板 at home in chapter 2, each with a ! till then); and a Flower Fruit Mountain game past the maths lessons finds it waiting on the timetable, as a lesson is done once its words are known. Its picture is two peaches, so its look-alikes are 二, 桃子 and 水果.
- **One thing at a time** (decided 7 November 2026, after playing it: chopping the senior pupil's logs, the count 一 was said just as the next log's question came up, two Chinese words at once with a number among its answers, and the question said 2 of 3 as you heard 一): nothing the game says or shows runs into a question. A count said as you do one thing after another is said in full and seen, then cleared, before the next one begins (`sayFully`); a question never opens over a message (`pictureQ` clears it first, `clearMessage` in js/helpers.js, which never announces a new task in the middle of things); and what you're told about something just done, or just before a question, gets its moment first: what you turned into, while you're it; bait on your hook, before you reel the fish in (`BAITED_MS`); how many animals you've found, once you've met one, not as its questions begin.
- **When you only hear the word, no two answers sound alike** apart from their tones (喝 hē and 河 hé): confusing, and a voice can't always make the tone clear. When reading, they can be answers together.
- **Answers are never look-alikes:** words whose pictures share their main thing (床 bed and 睡觉 to sleep, both a bed) or whose meanings overlap (家 home and 学校 school; 动物 animal and any animal) are never two answers in a question, and are in different rounds of matching when they can be. The word bank lists them (`alike`): when you add a word with a picture, list its look-alikes. The numbers and colours aren't look-alikes: telling them apart is the point.
- **How a word is made** (`MADE` in content/words.js): only where it's simple, useful and memorable: you get it in one look, with no history lesson, and every part is something you can picture, a word or a shape that looks like what it means (decided after playing it: the 艹 of 花, plants, meant nothing). Only where it genuinely helps you understand or remember the word, never for characters that just say the meaning again (朋友: friend + friend), never a story that isn't true, and nothing for a word with no clear story. Each part has its pinyin (as said in the word: a test checks the parts make the word's own), and the word they make has its own English, what it means, never what the parts add up to (decided 25 October 2026: 山 mountain + 羊 sheep = 山羊 goat, not a mountain sheep); a part you know as a word is green, and a card says where you've met a part before, in another word you know. It goes with the English, on a card of its own: straight after a new word's first card (How it's made ▸), then from the English button, which shows the English and then becomes How it's made. Its parts are never new words to catch, and every explanation goes to the native speaker (CHINESE.md).
- **Quizzes only use words the player has already caught**, including all wrong-answer options. Every picture must be unique and unambiguous. Answers show Chinese under the picture when listening (its characters), and each one's pinyin once you've answered (before, you could match the sound to the pinyin).
- **Never characters alone.** Wherever a question shows Chinese for you to recognise, its pinyin is with it (unless pinyin is switched off in Settings). When reading, the word shows characters and pinyin, and the answers are just pictures, so the pinyin can't be matched to itself.
- **Writing in the world is real Chinese.** Signs, tablets and blackboards say something real (words from the word bank, or a name), drawn as pixel characters (`HANZI` in js/art.js, written with `write()`; a new character needs a drawing there), never made-up squiggles. Word pictures have no writing in them, so they can't give an answer away.
- **Names of characters** (e.g. 老猴子 lǎo hóuzi · Old monkey) show Chinese, pinyin and English together, because names aren't vocabulary. So does the game's own name on the title screen (小猴子 xiǎo hóuzi, Little Monkey), and places' names (花果山 Huāguǒ Shān, Flower Fruit Mountain).
- **English beside Chinese always says what that Chinese means**, never something else next to it (the title screen had the area's name, Flower Fruit Mountain, under 小猴子, as if that were its meaning). Use plain descriptions, never invented nicknames.
- **Animal names:** the first meeting teaches the name on a new-word card and the encounter never shows the English. Later meetings show Chinese and pinyin with English on tap.
- **The old monkey must be spoken to first**; other characters point the player to him until then.

## Controls rules (keep these)
- D-pad: hold to walk, tap to turn on the spot, slide between directions without lifting. Text on the page must never be selectable.
- **A** = talk, look, choose. On cards, A always presses the main (green) button, marked with a red A badge.
- **B** = back or dismiss (blue B badge on the button it presses), and on the map, held as you walk, it runs, once you can (the area's `running`, such as once PE has taught you 跑; decided 28 October 2026): each step a third quicker (`RUN_STEP` in js/input.js), and with honey's energy too, about twice as fast as walking; the label under B says Run, and a tip points at it the first time. A tap of B still closes a message. B never opens the menu.
- **A or B pressed before a card or a line appeared** (while the game was busy), or just as it did (`READ_MS` in js/cards.js), doesn't press it, so pressing A again and again while waiting can't skip something unread. B waits a whole second (`B_READ_MS`) before it moves a line on or closes a card or a message that's just come up, as you might be holding or tapping it to run (decided 29 October 2026, after playing it).
- **When something ends, you can tell, and one more A never starts it again.** A conversation's last line says Done, not ▼ Next (when the game can be sure nothing comes after it: `showsMore` in js/world.js). A message that has come up since you last moved or turned is closed by A, as by B, never reaching past it (it's where the speech box was, and looks like it). And what you've just finished with rests (js/input.js): A at it does nothing, with its label blank, until you turn, take a step or 2 seconds pass, unless the one you're facing has something new for you, a ! or ? over them, and not what they've just said (`hasNews` in js/world.js), so a story that carries on with the same character flows; nor stepping stones or water you fish in, so you can hop or cast again at once (`justFinished` in js/input.js); nor where you're collecting more of the same, one at a time, such as the bowls from the cave's stack or the cow's fresh grass: there A takes the next at once, even with the message the last one left still up (anything else you're told just then, such as a new task, A closes first, never skipping it), and only once you have them all does it close the message and rest (`collectsMore` in js/world.js, `messageFirst` in js/input.js; decided 6 November 2026, after playing it: filling the senior pupil's buckets one at a time, the pool rested after each, so the next seemed not to come). And someone you've just finished with who has something more for you goes straight on to it, as you're there (the `goOn` step), so you're never told to go and see someone you're standing beside.
- **A controller** (js/gamepad.js) presses the same buttons, with the Xbox layout: the bottom button is A, the right one B, the left one BAG, the top one MAP, Start is MENU, Select (View) is TASKS, and the D-pad or left stick is the D-pad. Answers can be chosen with the D-pad (or arrow keys) and A as well as tapped; with nothing chosen, A shows the first answer and never answers for you. Once you've answered, the card is like any other: A presses Next, whatever you tap after (🔊 to hear the word again, or an answer), and the D-pad no longer chooses between its buttons.
- **Menu** and **Tasks** open the menu and your tasks: two small buttons under the D-pad, where a Game Boy has Start and Select, labelled MENU and TASKS (keyboard: M or Esc for the menu, T for your tasks). Pressing either again closes it. **Bag** and **Map** are two more of the same under A and B (decided 26 October 2026, after trying a thumbstick and four buttons, which were less liked): BAG opens your bag, and again closes it; MAP opens the map once you have one (js/map.js: till then it says you don't have one yet); both only while you're free to move about (keyboard: I and P). Both pages show your words, friends and shells at the top. There's no header or task box: the screen uses the room.
- **One layout, like a Game Boy Advance held sideways:** the screen in the middle, always 15 columns by 10 rows, the D-pad with Tasks and Menu under it on the left, A and B with Bag and Map under them on the right (A a little higher, like a GBA), and a power light just for looks. When the window is taller than it is wide (a phone held upright), a card asks you to turn the phone and the game waits.
- A/B badges appear only on a card's bottom action buttons and in the menu, never on things you tap directly (answers, words in sentences). The A badge is always on a button's top-left corner and the B badge on its top-right, tucked in so they never overlap a neighbouring button, and the button gives it room so it never covers a letter (on a small phone, `data-screen` on the page, set by fit() in js/main.js, and the cards' container queries on #modal).
- **A card with more than it shows** has ▼ More by its buttons (`moreCue` in js/cards.js) until you reach its end; tapping it, or the D-pad's down, scrolls the card, and up scrolls back (on a card whose D-pad moves between buttons, the button you choose comes into sight instead). The labels under A and B never move the buttons: a long one (more than 10 letters, such as How it's made) wraps onto two lines (decided 25 October 2026: on one line it looked squashed), and a button's words longer than 13 letters give just the first (Copy for Copy to a file), unless it has its own `data-label`.
- **Who's speaking** (decided 6 November 2026, from mock-ups): the speech box's name plate, their face (head and shoulders: `bustHTML` in js/art.js) in a circle and their name, in one thin pale-gold outline (css `--plate`), as tall as the face even when a long name's English goes under it, so the face's ring and the plate's round end are one (decided 6 November 2026, after playing it: the plate grew taller, a ring round a ring); on the map, a small gold arrow over them in place of their ! or ?, and a faint ring at their feet, while their line is up (`speakingNow` in js/draw.js; `talkWho`, cleared as the box closes).
- Your health shows at the top left while you're hurt (`#hud`), and goes while a message is up at the top of the screen, coming back as it goes (decided 7 November 2026, after playing it: they overlapped).
- Messages appear in a box at the bottom of the game screen (in a conversation, they wait in the speech box until A), or at the top straight after a speech box that was there, till you move or turn, so they don't jump from top to bottom (`messageTop` in js/draw.js), or when at the bottom it would cover you or the square you face, such as your float as you fish off the pier (`underMessage`). A new task is announced with a message, and a red dot stays on Tasks until you look at your tasks.
