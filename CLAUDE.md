# Little Monkey (小猴子): project notes for Claude Code

## About this project
A retro, Pokémon-style web game for learning Mandarin Chinese, themed on *Journey to the West*. The player is a baby monkey (Sun Wukong) who collects Chinese words like creatures, hears them again and again in simple sentences from the story's characters, and earns progress by practising. It's a vocabulary game: it never tests grammar.

- **DESIGN.md** is the plan and the source of truth for design decisions. Follow it. If a request conflicts with it, point that out before building.
- **index.html** is the game. It loads `css/game.css`, then the content in `content/`, then the engine in `js/`, in the order listed in index.html; each script can use what the ones before it define. Until they have set the game up, a start-up screen covers the page (the app's monkey and three dots: `loading` on `<html>`, taken off at the end of js/main.js), so the console never shows half-built while the code downloads. All pixel art and sound are generated in code.
- **content/** is the game's content, as data: `words.js` (the word bank: every word once, with pinyin, English, picture and the area it's collected in, and for a phrase, when you say it and what you answer with it; a word the area teaches `later`, such as 蜜蜂 till the bees, isn't counted till then; and its look-alikes, `alike`, such as 家 and 学校, both houses, which are never two answers in a question; and `MADE`, how a word is made: its parts and what they add up to, the clue inside a character, or the picture it was drawn from, shown with its English (`madeHTML` in js/cards.js); a part can name a word with it that has none of its own, so a card can say you've met it there, such as the 学 of 同学, in 学校), `pictures.js` (every word's 32 × 32 pixel picture, and the game's 16 × 16 icons, made by `tools/draw-pictures.js`: change a drawing there and run `npm run pictures`, never edit pictures.js itself; the game's text uses an emoji such as ⭐ and shows its pixel icon instead, and a test checks every emoji has one), `sprites.js` (the animals as they look walking on the map, 16 × 16, each with the way it `faces`, so it can be turned to look at you) and one file per area, such as `flower-fruit-mountain.js`. An area's `places` are the small maps you move between, each with a name, its own `music` if it has some (such as the cave's), its `gloom` if it's gloomy as under thick trees (but always in the daytime, never night: shade, sunbeams, mist and eyes, such as the pine wood's), its own `sounds` now and then, and its own `battleMusic` and `backdrop` for battles and animals' questions there (such as the pine wood's pines), its `exits` (ways to other places, which can stay shut until the story opens them, `if`, or not be there at all until it says, `when`; you walk into an open one even through a building, such as the school's door), its own `legend` if it needs letters that only mean something there (such as the classroom's, or the pine wood's long grass, where wolves live too), and its map; a position anywhere else in the file is in the first place unless it says another (`[x, y, 'place']` or `place:'id'`). Its `legend` says what each map letter is (how it looks, whether you can walk on it, the word it teaches, what A does (where what you collect has another word, such as a fruit tree's 水果, fruit, or the river's 河, A looks first, teaching it); a `ledge` you can only hop down (a bank two squares high, such as the hillside's, in one hop; walk up into one and you slip back down); the rock all round the mountain, `^`, its top seen from above, with `M` at its front, a cliff two squares high, as every cliff and bank is (a test checks), and `=` stone steps up them; a cliff can have a story `carving` cut into it, its squares together one panel, whose scene A plays (collected in Settings to watch again, as its scene's `watch` says); `onLook` steps for pressing A at it while the story says; `becomes`, what it turns into once the story says, such as the rocks rolling away or a path the troop clears; a single square can also change for good with the `change` step, kept in the save, such as a dead pine you knock down with your stick, the `hit` step (it counts only while the map still has there what changed, so a map can be redrawn); `collect` gets things for a request one at a time, counting them aloud, and an `onLook` part with `sparkle` sparkles while it matches; `markOut` changes the story while you watch the ground it changes being marked out, a plot's stakes and string, then the ground, its sign and path, so a plot never just appears; `plant` does the same for something planted, each square coming up out of the ground in turn, such as the flowers in front of the school; a `gather` with `picked` shows what you've picked on a card like a word's, such as 红色的花 (a red flower), with its own picture: a picture for a few words is drawn in tools/draw-pictures.js like a word's, named as the content writes them, with spaces). Trees, the fruit trees, your stone, the rocks, the stall, the practice post and the cave's stone tablet are big objects, drawn bigger than their squares (`OBJECT_ART` in `js/art.js`): a block of forest two squares by two is one big tree, and a row of the same rock or stall letter is one object as wide as the row (the stall is `HHH`); the looks are in `js/art.js` and the special actions in `js/world.js`. Its `characters` stand on the map (one can hold something, `holds`, and swing it now and then as it `practises`, such as the little monkeys in training on the hillside with their sticks). Its `talk` scripts say what each character does: the first part whose `if` matches runs its list of steps (`STEPS` in `js/world.js`; a step with an `if` only runs if it matches; `lesson` is a lesson with the teacher: the next new word of a series in the area's `lessons`, such as the numbers (kind `count`) or the colours (kind `paint`: each colour paints a part of the school, its `paint`, in its scene), one a lesson, each once the last is known, the series taking turns; or else practising your words as the practice post did; `practiseAt` says where the game tells you to practise; `wordGame` is a character's word game, with a present if you win, such as the `shells` step's). Its `requests` are characters asking you to bring something (run by `request()` in `js/world.js`), its `events` happen by themselves when their `if` matches, and its `counters` are numbers that text and conditions can use (one counting words you know can say what it `called` each, such as the meadow's words, which the old monkey wants you to learn). The task box (`tasks`, and `hints` when there are none), the area `checklist` (an item with `show` only appears once you've found it; the exit test opens once everything else is done) and the ! and ? `bubbles` over characters are content too, as are its `food` (what each fruit in your bag does: `heal` or `helps`; fruit trees' `pick` in the legend, and the `bag` step, fill it), its `animals` (friends you make by answering their questions: hiding in tall grass, anywhere or in one place's only, `hides`, such as the fox on the hillside, or at their own spot, `at`, such as up a tree or in a clearing, where you can't walk through them; an animal's `chapter` says which chapter's animals it's counted with, such as the deer in the pine wood, and a counter of animals can count one place's, `place`; `animalsPlay`, what one says as it comes out to play, before its questions, with what's happening under it), its `enemies` and `battles` (wild creatures that jump out of `wild` ground to fight, run by `js/battle.js`: by chance, from the ground's `wild` list, but never the same three times in a row unless it's all that lives there), the area's `name`, what happens when you first `begin`, the opening (`intro`) and other `scenes` told like it (such as becoming king, played by the `scene` step; their pictures are `SCENE_ART` in `js/draw.js`, named by a scene's `art` when several share them, such as painting each part of the school; one with `watch` can be watched again from Settings), the `storyEnd` card, the `exit` texts and where you're put back, the `fish`, `animalsAppear` (when animals start jumping out), its `tips` about Chinese (each shown once, on a card, just before an animal's questions the first time one of its word plays with you, such as the goat's: one character, one syllable), its `sign` (whose `made` shows how your home's name is made, on a card like a word's, once you know its parts: `nameCard` in js/cards.js), its `sparkles` (on things whose word you don't know yet; one with no word sparkles while its `if` matches, such as the sign till you've read it), and what's `hidden` off the paths (shells or a fruit at a tree or a boulder, found by pressing A at it, once, kept in the save's `finds`, with a tiny glint now and then). The engine holds nothing specific to an area; each area file is the place to change it. In content text, `{汉字}` is shown as 汉字 (English) (with the word's first meaning; `{石头|stones}` gives the English that fits the sentence; in your tasks, the checklist and hints, a word you haven't caught is just its English; at most two in a sentence, never side by side, as a test checks), `{counter}` as its number, and `{left:counter}` as the words a counter of words you know still waits for, such as "the river and a bird". Every line must be said somewhere (a test checks). Chinese sentences are written with a space between words (`'我 饿 了 。'`), and every word must be in the word bank; `npm test` checks this and more.
- **Version 8**, the single-file original built in a Claude chat, is in git history as `little-monkey.html` (first commit). It was deleted once its save format was replaced.
- **The app:** `manifest.webmanifest` lets the game be added to a phone's home screen, opening full screen and sideways; `sw.js` (the service worker) always loads the newest version when there's a connection (on a slow one, the copy after 3 seconds) and keeps a copy for offline from the very first visit, including every voice clip (the game sends it the list of its files and clips). The copy is always one version, whole: a new version's files go into it together once the game has loaded, and the old and new versions never mix. In a phone's browser, the title screen offers Play full screen (`js/fullscreen.js`): on Android it goes full screen and sideways, with Install the app when the browser offers it; on an iPhone, which can't do either for a web page, a card shows how to Add to Home Screen. Never once it's opened as the app. The icons in `icons/` are made from the game's monkey by `npm run icons`.
- **Three games (save slots)** are on the title screen. Game 1 is kept in the browser under `xiaohouzi-v2`, where the one game always was; Games 2 and 3 under `xiaohouzi-v2-game2` and `-game3`; `xiaohouzi-v2-slots` remembers which game you're playing and when each was last played. Each game keeps its own settings. Copy to a file and Load from a file move a game between devices; deleting keeps a copy in the browser.
- **The save** (`js/state.js`) has a version number. When the format changes: raise `SAVE_VERSION`, add a step to `upgradeSave()` that converts the previous version, and add a test to `tests/save.test.js`. The game keeps a copy of each old save in the browser before converting it (`xiaohouzi-v2-backup-v2` and so on), and never overwrites a broken save.

## Tools
- `npm test` runs the automated tests (Node's built-in test runner; there are no packages to install).
- `npm start` serves the game at http://127.0.0.1:8000. The preview pane's "game" configuration does the same.
- `npm run pictures` remakes content/pictures.js, the word pictures, from the drawings in tools/draw-pictures.js (a test checks it's up to date). Each drawing is made of shapes (ovals, rectangles, polygons and strokes), shaded and outlined for you; every picture must be different, so a new word needs a new drawing.
- `npm run icons` remakes the app icons in icons/ from the monkey in js/art.js (a test checks they're up to date).
- `npm run chinese` writes CHINESE.md: all the game's Chinese with pinyin and English, for a native speaker to check, plus new sentences waiting to be checked (DRAFTS in tools/chinese-sheet.js). Run it after changing any Chinese; a test checks it's up to date.
- `npm run voices` makes a voice clip for every piece of Chinese in the content (audio/<voice>/, listed in content/voices.js), said by the area's `voices`: Google's Standard A (a woman, made on GitHub, which has Google's key) for a word said on its own, Yunxi (a young man, in his chat style) for lines, and Yunze (an older man) for the old monkey's lines; all at normal speed, and the game skips any silence around a clip as it plays it. It only makes what's missing and removes clips nothing says, so run it after changing any Chinese; a test checks every clip is made, and the playthrough test checks everything the game says has one. It needs the Azure key in voice-key.txt. `SAY_AS` in tools/make-voices.js gives the voice the pinyin for a word it reads wrongly (Google's voice is checked: if it takes no notice, it says a word that sounds the same, `google`, or another voice, `by`, says it).

It needs the Azure key in voice-key.txt, which stays on this computer (.gitignore keeps it off GitHub; a test checks). Never print or commit the key.

## Learning design rules (decided through playtesting; keep these)
- **One new word at a time.** No conversation, action or encounter teaches two new words back to back.
- **New-word cards** show the picture, characters, pinyin, sound and English. English is shown by default on the first card only; after that it is behind an English button. A card in a conversation (a character teaching the word, a reply, the shop) doesn't say the word again, so the conversation flows; 🔊 Hear it is there. A word you discover yourself (looking, stepping on something, picking it, meeting an animal) is said straight away.
- **Sentences:** pinyin over every word (a global setting can turn it off), never smaller than 10.5px, so its tone marks can be read on a small phone. Words the player has learned are green. The word being taught right now is bold green. Everything else is grey and "just for listening". Tapping any word shows its pinyin and meaning.
- **A sentence's English** is shown underneath the first time it appears; after that it is behind an English button.
- **Never test grammar.** A task involving a sentence always shows the whole sentence in Chinese (with pinyin) and English, with one word missing, and you choose the missing word. Never ask players to put words in order. The choices follow the word's level: its picture with the Chinese while it's new (Caught), Chinese and pinyin once recognised. A wrong choice shows the right one and play carries on.
- **Reply choices** show Chinese, pinyin and English every time, so replying is input, not a test, under what you're answering: the line just said (or what's happening, with a picture, such as a gift), put in plain English with what to do ("The hungry monkey thanks you. What do you say back?"). Settings, English on replies, turns the English off for players who know some Chinese. Any choice can be made; one that doesn't fit is shown with why (each phrase's `use`, in words.js) and the one that does, which your monkey then says, so the conversation makes sense.
- **In English UI text** (tasks, messages, notes), a word you know can be written as 汉字 (English), e.g. "Give the 水 (water) to the thirsty monkey", but only two at most in a sentence (best one: the word the sentence is about), never two side by side, and never a describing word on a word ("a red 花 (flower)", not "a 红色 (red) 花 (flower)", which reads as Chinese without its 的). Everything else stays plain English, so a sentence never looks like Chinese in English word order. Tasks for words not yet learned are written in plain English.
- **When you only hear the word, no two answers sound alike** apart from their tones (喝 hē and 河 hé): confusing, and a voice can't always make the tone clear. When reading, they can be answers together.
- **Answers are never look-alikes:** words whose pictures share their main thing (床 bed and 睡觉 to sleep, both a bed) or whose meanings overlap (家 home and 学校 school; 动物 animal and any animal) are never two answers in a question, and are in different rounds of matching when they can be. The word bank lists them (`alike`): when you add a word with a picture, list its look-alikes. The numbers and colours aren't look-alikes: telling them apart is the point.
- **How a word is made** (`MADE` in content/words.js): only where it genuinely helps you understand or remember the word, never for characters that just say the meaning again (朋友: friend + friend), never a story that isn't true, and nothing for a word with no clear story. Each part has its pinyin (as said in the word: a test checks the parts make the word's own), a part you know as a word is green, and a card says where you've met a part before, in another word you know. It goes with the English (on the first card, then behind the English button), its parts are never new words to catch, and every explanation goes to the native speaker (CHINESE.md).
- **Quizzes only use words the player has already caught**, including all wrong-answer options. Every picture must be unique and unambiguous. Answers show Chinese under the picture when listening (its characters), and each one's pinyin once you've answered (before, you could match the sound to the pinyin).
- **Never characters alone.** Wherever a question shows Chinese for you to recognise, its pinyin is with it (unless pinyin is switched off in Settings). When reading, the word shows characters and pinyin, and the answers are just pictures, so the pinyin can't be matched to itself.
- **Writing in the world is real Chinese.** Signs, tablets and blackboards say something real (words from the word bank, or a name), drawn as pixel characters (`HANZI` in js/art.js, written with `write()`; a new character needs a drawing there), never made-up squiggles. Word pictures have no writing in them, so they can't give an answer away.
- **Names of characters** (e.g. 老猴子 lǎo hóuzi · Old monkey) show Chinese, pinyin and English together, because names aren't vocabulary. So does the game's own name on the title screen (小猴子 xiǎo hóuzi, Little Monkey), and places' names (花果山 Huāguǒ Shān, Flower Fruit Mountain).
- **English beside Chinese always says what that Chinese means**, never something else next to it (the title screen had the area's name, Flower Fruit Mountain, under 小猴子, as if that were its meaning). Use plain descriptions, never invented nicknames.
- **Animal names:** the first meeting teaches the name on a new-word card and the encounter never shows the English. Later meetings show Chinese and pinyin with English on tap.
- **The old monkey must be spoken to first**; other characters point the player to him until then.

## Controls rules (keep these)
- D-pad: hold to walk, tap to turn on the spot, slide between directions without lifting. Text on the page must never be selectable.
- **A** = talk, look, choose. On cards, A always presses the main (green) button, marked with a red A badge.
- **B** = back or dismiss only (blue B badge on the button it presses). B never opens the menu.
- **A or B pressed before a card or a line appeared** (while the game was busy), or just as it did (`READ_MS` in js/cards.js), doesn't press it, so pressing A again and again while waiting can't skip something unread.
- **When something ends, you can tell, and one more A never starts it again.** A conversation's last line says Done, not ▼ Next (when the game can be sure nothing comes after it: `showsMore` in js/world.js). A message that has come up since you last moved or turned is closed by A, as by B, never reaching past it (it's where the speech box was, and looks like it). And what you've just finished with rests (js/input.js): A at it does nothing, with its label blank, until you turn, take a step or 2 seconds pass, unless the one you're facing has something new for you, a ! or ? over them, and not what they've just said (`hasNews` in js/world.js), so a story that carries on with the same character flows.
- **A controller** (js/gamepad.js) presses the same buttons, with the Xbox layout: the bottom button is A, the right one B, Start is MENU, Select (View) is TASKS, and the D-pad or left stick is the D-pad. Answers can be chosen with the D-pad (or arrow keys) and A as well as tapped; with nothing chosen, A shows the first answer and never answers for you.
- **Menu** and **Tasks** open the menu and your tasks: two small buttons under the D-pad, where a Game Boy has Start and Select, labelled MENU and TASKS (keyboard: M or Esc for the menu, T for your tasks). Pressing either again closes it. Both pages show your words, friends and shells at the top. There's no header or task box: the screen uses the room.
- **One layout, like a Game Boy Advance held sideways:** the screen in the middle, always 15 columns by 10 rows, the D-pad with Tasks and Menu under it on the left, A and B on the right (A a little higher, like a GBA), and a power light and a speaker just for looks. When the window is taller than it is wide (a phone held upright), a card asks you to turn the phone and the game waits.
- A/B badges appear only on a card's bottom action buttons and in the menu, never on things you tap directly (answers, words in sentences). The A badge is always on a button's top-left corner and the B badge on its top-right, tucked in so they never overlap a neighbouring button, and the button gives it room so it never covers a letter (on a small phone, `data-screen` on the page, set by fit() in js/main.js, and the cards' container queries on #modal).
- **A card with more than it shows** has ▼ More by its buttons (`moreCue` in js/cards.js) until you reach its end; tapping it, or the D-pad's down, scrolls the card, and up scrolls back (on a card whose D-pad moves between buttons, the button you choose comes into sight instead). The labels under A and B stay on one line and never move the buttons: a long one is a little smaller, and a button's words longer than that give just the first (Copy for Copy to a file), unless it has its own `data-label`.
- Messages appear in a box at the bottom of the game screen (in a conversation, they wait in the speech box until A). A new task is announced with a message, and a red dot stays on Tasks until you look at your tasks.
