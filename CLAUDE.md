# Little Monkey (小猴子): project notes for Claude Code

## About this project
A retro, Pokémon-style web game for learning Mandarin Chinese, themed on *Journey to the West*. The player is a baby monkey (Sun Wukong) who collects Chinese words like creatures, hears them again and again in simple sentences from the story's characters, and earns progress by practising. It's a vocabulary game: it never tests grammar.

- **DESIGN.md** is the plan and the source of truth for design decisions. Follow it. If a request conflicts with it, point that out before building.
- **index.html** is the game. It loads `css/game.css`, then the content in `content/`, then the engine in `js/`, in the order listed in index.html; each script can use what the ones before it define. All pixel art and sound are generated in code.
- **content/** is the game's content, as data: `words.js` (the word bank: every word once, with pinyin, English, picture and the area it's collected in), `pictures.js` (every word's 32 × 32 pixel picture, and the game's 16 × 16 icons, made by `tools/draw-pictures.js`: change a drawing there and run `npm run pictures`, never edit pictures.js itself; the game's text uses an emoji such as ⭐ and shows its pixel icon instead, and a test checks every emoji has one), `sprites.js` (the animals as they look walking on the map, 16 × 16, each with the way it `faces`, so it can be turned to look at you) and one file per area, such as `flower-fruit-mountain.js`. An area's `places` are the small maps you move between, each with a name, its own `music` if it has some (such as the cave's), its `gloom` if it's gloomy as under thick trees (but always in the daytime, never night: shade, sunbeams, mist and eyes, such as the pine wood's), its own `sounds` now and then, and its own `battleMusic` and `backdrop` for battles and animals' questions there (such as the pine wood's pines), its `exits` (ways to other places, which can stay shut until the story opens them, `if`, or not be there at all until it says, `when`; you walk into an open one even through a building, such as the school's door), its own `legend` if it needs letters that only mean something there (such as the classroom's, or the pine wood's long grass, where wolves live too), and its map; a position anywhere else in the file is in the first place unless it says another (`[x, y, 'place']` or `place:'id'`). Its `legend` says what each map letter is (how it looks, whether you can walk on it, the word it teaches, what A does; a `ledge` you can only hop down; `onLook` steps for pressing A at it while the story says; `becomes`, what it turns into once the story says, such as the rocks rolling away or a path the troop clears; a single square can also change for good with the `change` step, kept in the save, such as a dead pine you knock down with your stick, the `hit` step (it counts only while the map still has there what changed, so a map can be redrawn); `collect` gets things for a request one at a time, counting them aloud, and an `onLook` part with `sparkle` sparkles while it matches). Trees, the fruit trees, your stone, the rocks, the stall and the practice post are big objects, drawn bigger than their squares (`OBJECT_ART` in `js/art.js`): a block of forest two squares by two is one big tree, and a row of the same rock or stall letter is one object as wide as the row (the stall is `HHH`); the looks are in `js/art.js` and the special actions in `js/world.js`. Its `talk` scripts say what each character does: the first part whose `if` matches runs its list of steps (`STEPS` in `js/world.js`; `lesson` is a lesson with the teacher: the next new word of a series in the area's `lessons`, such as the numbers (kind `count`) or the colours (kind `paint`: each colour paints a part of the school, its `paint`, in its scene), one a lesson, each once the last is known, the series taking turns; or else practising your words as the practice post did; `practiseAt` says where the game tells you to practise). Its `requests` are characters asking you to bring something (run by `request()` in `js/world.js`), its `events` happen by themselves when their `if` matches, and its `counters` are numbers that text and conditions can use. The task box (`tasks`, and `hints` when there are none), the area `checklist` (an item with `show` only appears once you've found it; the exit test opens once everything else is done) and the ! and ? `bubbles` over characters are content too, as are its `food` (what each fruit in your bag does: `heal` or `helps`; fruit trees' `pick` in the legend, and the `bag` step, fill it), its `animals` (friends you make by answering their questions: hiding in tall grass, or at their own spot, `at`, such as up a tree or in a clearing, where you can't walk through them; an animal's `chapter` says which chapter's animals it's counted with, such as the deer in the pine wood), its `enemies` and `battles` (wild creatures that jump out of `wild` ground to fight, run by `js/battle.js`), the area's `name`, what happens when you first `begin`, the opening (`intro`) and other `scenes` told like it (such as becoming king, played by the `scene` step; their pictures are `SCENE_ART` in `js/draw.js`, named by a scene's `art` when several share them, such as painting each part of the school; one with `watch` can be watched again from Settings), the `storyEnd` card, the `exit` texts and where you're put back, the `fish`, and `animalsAppear` (when animals start jumping out). The engine holds nothing specific to an area; each area file is the place to change it. In content text, `{汉字}` is shown as 汉字 (English) and `{counter}` as its number. Every line must be said somewhere (a test checks). Chinese sentences are written with a space between words (`'我 饿 了 。'`), and every word must be in the word bank; `npm test` checks this and more.
- **Version 8**, the single-file original built in a Claude chat, is in git history as `little-monkey.html` (first commit). It was deleted once its save format was replaced.
- **The app:** `manifest.webmanifest` lets the game be added to a phone's home screen, opening full screen and sideways; `sw.js` (the service worker) always loads the newest version when there's a connection (on a slow one, the copy after 3 seconds) and keeps a copy for offline from the very first visit, including every voice clip (the game sends it the list of its files and clips). In a phone's browser, the title screen offers Play full screen (`js/fullscreen.js`): on Android it goes full screen and sideways, with Install the app when the browser offers it; on an iPhone, which can't do either for a web page, a card shows how to Add to Home Screen. Never once it's opened as the app. The icons in `icons/` are made from the game's monkey by `npm run icons`.
- **Three games (save slots)** are on the title screen. Game 1 is kept in the browser under `xiaohouzi-v2`, where the one game always was; Games 2 and 3 under `xiaohouzi-v2-game2` and `-game3`; `xiaohouzi-v2-slots` remembers which game you're playing and when each was last played. Each game keeps its own settings. Copy to a file and Load from a file move a game between devices; deleting keeps a copy in the browser.
- **The save** (`js/state.js`) has a version number. When the format changes: raise `SAVE_VERSION`, add a step to `upgradeSave()` that converts the previous version, and add a test to `tests/save.test.js`. The game keeps a copy of each old save in the browser before converting it (`xiaohouzi-v2-backup-v2` and so on), and never overwrites a broken save.

## Tools
- `npm test` runs the automated tests (Node's built-in test runner; there are no packages to install).
- `npm start` serves the game at http://127.0.0.1:8000. The preview pane's "game" configuration does the same.
- `npm run pictures` remakes content/pictures.js, the word pictures, from the drawings in tools/draw-pictures.js (a test checks it's up to date). Each drawing is made of shapes (ovals, rectangles, polygons and strokes), shaded and outlined for you; every picture must be different, so a new word needs a new drawing.
- `npm run icons` remakes the app icons in icons/ from the monkey in js/art.js (a test checks they're up to date).
- `npm run chinese` writes CHINESE.md: all the game's Chinese with pinyin and English, for a native speaker to check, plus new sentences waiting to be checked (DRAFTS in tools/chinese-sheet.js). Run it after changing any Chinese; a test checks it's up to date.
- `npm run voices` makes a voice clip for every piece of Chinese in the content (audio/<voice>/, listed in content/voices.js), said by the area's `voices`: Google's Standard A (a woman, made on GitHub, which has Google's key) for a word said on its own, Yunxi (a young man, in his chat style) for lines, and Yunze (an older man) for the old monkey's lines; all at normal speed, and the game skips any silence around a clip as it plays it. It only makes what's missing and removes clips nothing says, so run it after changing any Chinese; a test checks every clip is made, and the playthrough test checks everything the game says has one. It needs the Azure key in voice-key.txt. `SAY_AS` in tools/make-voices.js gives the voice the pinyin for a word it reads wrongly.
- **Voice clips on GitHub:** the Make voice clips job (.github/workflows/voices.yml) makes any missing clips with the keys kept in GitHub's secrets (GOOGLE_TTS_KEY, and AZURE_TTS_KEY with AZURE_TTS_REGION for Azure's voices), when the `voices` branch is pushed or by hand from the Actions tab. It saves them back on that branch; merge it into main to use them. Useful when away from the computer with voice-key.txt.
- `npm run try-voices` makes the voice test: tricky words and lines in a few of Azure's Mandarin voices, to hear at http://127.0.0.1:8000/tools/try-voices.html beside the device's own voice. It needs the Azure key in voice-key.txt, which stays on this computer (.gitignore keeps it off GitHub; a test checks). Never print or commit the key.
- TESTING.md has the checks to do by hand: a quick check after every change and a full playthrough of Area 1.
- The screens gallery, http://127.0.0.1:8000/tools/screens.html (with `npm start`), shows every screen side by side at phone size, each the real game with a pretend save. ## Learning design rules (decided through playtesting; keep these)
- **One new word at a time.** No conversation, action or encounter teaches two new words back to back.
- **New-word cards** show the picture, characters, pinyin, sound and English. English is shown by default on the first card only; after that it is behind an English button. A card in a conversation (a character teaching the word, a reply, the shop) doesn't say the word again, so the conversation flows; 🔊 Hear it is there. A word you discover yourself (looking, stepping on something, picking it, meeting an animal) is said straight away.
- **Sentences:** pinyin over every word (a global setting can turn it off). Words the player has learned are green. The word being taught right now is bold green. Everything else is grey and "just for listening". Tapping any word shows its pinyin and meaning.
- **A sentence's English** is shown underneath the first time it appears; after that it is behind an English button.
- **Never test grammar.** A task involving a sentence always shows the whole sentence in Chinese (with pinyin) and English, with one word missing, and you choose the missing word. Never ask players to put words in order. The choices follow the word's level: its picture with the Chinese while it's new (Caught), Chinese and pinyin once recognised. A wrong choice shows the right one and play carries on.
- **Reply choices** show Chinese, pinyin and English every time, so replying is input, not a test. Settings, English on replies, turns the English off for players who know some Chinese. Any choice is fine; the game says which fits best.
- **In English UI text** (tasks, messages, notes), a Chinese word is always written as 汉字 (English), e.g. "Give the 水 (water) to the thirsty monkey". Tasks for words not yet learned are written in plain English.
- **When you only hear the word, no two answers sound alike** apart from their tones (喝 hē and 河 hé): confusing, and a voice can't always make the tone clear. When reading, they can be answers together.
- **Quizzes only use words the player has already caught**, including all wrong-answer options. Every picture must be unique and unambiguous. Answers show Chinese under the picture when listening (its characters), and each one's pinyin once you've answered (before, you could match the sound to the pinyin).
- **Never characters alone.** Wherever a question shows Chinese for you to recognise, its pinyin is with it (unless pinyin is switched off in Settings). When reading, the word shows characters and pinyin, and the answers are just pictures, so the pinyin can't be matched to itself.
- **Names of characters** (e.g. 老猴子 lǎo hóuzi · Old monkey) show Chinese, pinyin and English together, because names aren't vocabulary. Use plain descriptions, never invented nicknames.
- **Animal names:** the first meeting teaches the name on a new-word card and the encounter never shows the English. Later meetings show Chinese and pinyin with English on tap.
- **The old monkey must be spoken to first**; other characters point the player to him until then.

## Controls rules (keep these)
- D-pad: hold to walk, tap to turn on the spot, slide between directions without lifting. Text on the page must never be selectable.
- **A** = talk, look, choose. On cards, A always presses the main (green) button, marked with a red A badge.
- **B** = back or dismiss only (blue B badge on the button it presses). B never opens the menu.
- **A controller** (js/gamepad.js) presses the same buttons, with the Xbox layout: the bottom button is A, the right one B, Start is MENU, Select (View) is TASKS, and the D-pad or left stick is the D-pad. Answers can be chosen with the D-pad (or arrow keys) and A as well as tapped; with nothing chosen, A shows the first answer and never answers for you.
- **Menu** and **Tasks** open the menu and your tasks: two small buttons under the D-pad, where a Game Boy has Start and Select, labelled MENU and TASKS (keyboard: M or Esc for the menu, T for your tasks). Pressing either again closes it. Both pages show your words, friends and shells at the top. There's no header or task box: the screen uses the room.
- **One layout, like a Game Boy Advance held sideways:** the screen in the middle, always 15 columns by 10 rows, the D-pad with Tasks and Menu under it on the left, A and B on the right (A a little higher, like a GBA), and a power light and a speaker just for looks. When the window is taller than it is wide (a phone held upright), a card asks you to turn the phone and the game waits.
- A/B badges appear only on a card's bottom action buttons and in the menu, never on things you tap directly (answers, words in sentences). The A badge is always on a button's top-left corner and the B badge on its top-right, tucked in so they never overlap a neighbouring button.
- Messages appear in a box at the bottom of the game screen (in a conversation, they wait in the speech box until A). A new task is announced with a message, and a red dot stays on Tasks until you look at your tasks.
