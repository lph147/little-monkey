# Little Monkey (小猴子): project notes for Claude Code

## About this project
A retro, Pokémon-style web game for learning Mandarin Chinese, themed on *Journey to the West*. The player is a baby monkey (Sun Wukong) who collects Chinese words like creatures, hears them again and again in simple sentences from the story's characters, and earns progress by practising. It's a vocabulary game: it never tests grammar.

- **DESIGN.md** is the plan and the source of truth for design decisions. Follow it. If a request conflicts with it, point that out before building.
- **index.html** is the game. It loads `css/game.css`, then the content in `content/`, then the engine in `js/`, in the order listed in index.html; each script can use what the ones before it define. Until they have set the game up, a start-up screen covers the page (the app's monkey and three dots: `loading` on `<html>`, taken off at the end of js/main.js), so the console never shows half-built while the code downloads. All pixel art and sound are generated in code.

## Learning design rules (decided through playtesting; keep these)
- **One new word at a time.** No conversation, action or encounter teaches two new words back to back. The one exception is the school's first lessons (decided 24 October 2026): the first lesson of each kind teaches three words, one after another, each with its own card and questions before the next, then a mix (四 五 六, then 红色 黄色 白色), so two lessons open up chapter 1. Every lesson after that teaches one.
- **New-word cards** show the picture, characters, pinyin, sound and English. English is shown by default on the first card only; after that it is behind an English button. A card in a conversation (a character teaching the word, a reply, the shop) doesn't say the word again, so the conversation flows; 🔊 Hear it is there. A word you discover yourself (looking, stepping on something, picking it, meeting an animal) is said straight away.
- **Sentences:** pinyin over every word (a global setting can turn it off), never smaller than 10.5px, so its tone marks can be read on a small phone. Words the player has learned are green. The word being taught right now is bold green. Everything else is grey and "just for listening". Tapping any word shows its pinyin and meaning.
- **A sentence's English** is shown underneath the first time it appears; after that it is behind an English button.
- **Never test grammar.** A task involving a sentence always shows the whole sentence in Chinese (with pinyin) and English, with one word missing, and you choose the missing word. Never ask players to put words in order. The choices follow the word's level: its picture with the Chinese while it's new (Caught), Chinese and pinyin once recognised. A wrong choice shows the right one and play carries on.
- **Reply choices** show Chinese, pinyin and English every time, so replying is input, not a test, under what you're answering: the line just said (or what's happening, with a picture, such as a gift), put in plain English with what to do ("The hungry monkey thanks you. What do you say back?"). Settings, English on replies, turns the English off for players who know some Chinese. Any choice can be made; one that doesn't fit is shown with why (each phrase's `use`, in words.js) and the one that does, which your monkey then says, so the conversation makes sense.
- **In English UI text** (tasks, messages, notes), a word you know can be written as 汉字 (English), e.g. "Give the 水 (water) to the thirsty monkey", but only two at most in a sentence (best one: the word the sentence is about), never two side by side, and never a describing word on a word ("a red 花 (flower)", not "a 红色 (red) 花 (flower)", which reads as Chinese without its 的). Everything else stays plain English, so a sentence never looks like Chinese in English word order. Tasks for words not yet learned are written in plain English.
- **When you only hear the word, no two answers sound alike** apart from their tones (喝 hē and 河 hé): confusing, and a voice can't always make the tone clear. When reading, they can be answers together.
- **Answers are never look-alikes:** words whose pictures share their main thing (床 bed and 睡觉 to sleep, both a bed) or whose meanings overlap (家 home and 学校 school; 动物 animal and any animal) are never two answers in a question, and are in different rounds of matching when they can be. The word bank lists them (`alike`): when you add a word with a picture, list its look-alikes. The numbers and colours aren't look-alikes: telling them apart is the point.
- **How a word is made** (`MADE` in content/words.js): only where it's simple, useful and memorable: you get it in one look, with no history lesson, and every part is something you can picture, a word or a shape that looks like what it means (decided after playing it: the 艹 of 花, plants, meant nothing). Only where it genuinely helps you understand or remember the word, never for characters that just say the meaning again (朋友: friend + friend), never a story that isn't true, and nothing for a word with no clear story. Each part has its pinyin (as said in the word: a test checks the parts make the word's own), and the word they make has its own English, what it means, never what the parts add up to (decided 25 October 2026: 山 mountain + 羊 sheep = 山羊 goat, not a mountain sheep); a part you know as a word is green, and a card says where you've met a part before, in another word you know. It goes with the English, on a card of its own: straight after a new word's first card (How it's made ▸), then from the English button, which shows the English and then becomes How it's made. Its parts are never new words to catch, and every explanation goes to the native speaker (CHINESE.md).
- **Quizzes only use words the player has already caught**, including all wrong-answer options. Every picture must be unique and unambiguous. Answers show Chinese under the picture when listening (its characters), and each one's pinyin once you've answered (before, you could match the sound to the pinyin).
- **Never characters alone.** Wherever a question shows Chinese for you to recognise, its pinyin is with it (unless pinyin is switched off in Settings). When reading, the word shows characters and pinyin, and the answers are just pictures, so the pinyin can't be matched to itself.
- **Writing in the world is real Chinese.** Signs, tablets and blackboards say something real (words from the word bank, or a name), drawn as pixel characters (`HANZI` in js/art.js, written with `write()`; a new character needs a drawing there), never made-up squiggles. Word pictures have no writing in them, so they can't give an answer away.
- **Names of characters** (e.g. 老猴子 lǎo hóuzi · Old monkey) show Chinese, pinyin and English together, because names aren't vocabulary. So does the game's own name on the title screen (小猴子 xiǎo hóuzi, Little Monkey), and places' names (花果山 Huāguǒ Shān, Flower Fruit Mountain).
- **English beside Chinese always says what that Chinese means**, never something else next to it (the title screen had the area's name, Flower Fruit Mountain, under 小猴子, as if that were its meaning). Use plain descriptions, never invented nicknames.
- **Animal names:** the first meeting teaches the name on a new-word card and the encounter never shows the English. Later meetings show Chinese and pinyin with English on tap.
- **The old monkey must be spoken to first**; other characters point the player to him until then.

## Controls rules (keep these)
- D-pad: hold to walk, tap to turn on the spot, slide between directions without lifting. Text on the page must never be selectable.
- **A** = talk, look, choose. On cards, A always presses the main (green) button, marked with a red A badge.
- **B** = back or dismiss (blue B badge on the button it presses), and on the map, held as you walk, it runs, once you can (the area's `running`, such as once PE has taught you 跑; decided 28 October 2026): each step a third quicker (`RUN_STEP` in js/input.js), and with honey's energy too, about twice as fast as walking; the label under B says Run, and a tip points at it the first time. A tap of B still closes a message. B never opens the menu.
- **A or B pressed before a card or a line appeared** (while the game was busy), or just as it did (`READ_MS` in js/cards.js), doesn't press it, so pressing A again and again while waiting can't skip something unread.
- **When something ends, you can tell, and one more A never starts it again.** A conversation's last line says Done, not ▼ Next (when the game can be sure nothing comes after it: `showsMore` in js/world.js). A message that has come up since you last moved or turned is closed by A, as by B, never reaching past it (it's where the speech box was, and looks like it). And what you've just finished with rests (js/input.js): A at it does nothing, with its label blank, until you turn, take a step or 2 seconds pass, unless the one you're facing has something new for you, a ! or ? over them, and not what they've just said (`hasNews` in js/world.js), so a story that carries on with the same character flows.
- **A controller** (js/gamepad.js) presses the same buttons, with the Xbox layout: the bottom button is A, the right one B, the left one BAG, the top one MAP, Start is MENU, Select (View) is TASKS, and the D-pad or left stick is the D-pad. Answers can be chosen with the D-pad (or arrow keys) and A as well as tapped; with nothing chosen, A shows the first answer and never answers for you. Once you've answered, the card is like any other: A presses Next, whatever you tap after (🔊 to hear the word again, or an answer), and the D-pad no longer chooses between its buttons.
- **Menu** and **Tasks** open the menu and your tasks: two small buttons under the D-pad, where a Game Boy has Start and Select, labelled MENU and TASKS (keyboard: M or Esc for the menu, T for your tasks). Pressing either again closes it. **Bag** and **Map** are two more of the same under A and B (decided 26 October 2026, after trying a thumbstick and four buttons, which were less liked): BAG opens your bag, and again closes it; MAP will open the map (for now, it says it's coming soon); both only while you're free to move about (keyboard: I and P). Both pages show your words, friends and shells at the top. There's no header or task box: the screen uses the room.
- **One layout, like a Game Boy Advance held sideways:** the screen in the middle, always 15 columns by 10 rows, the D-pad with Tasks and Menu under it on the left, A and B with Bag and Map under them on the right (A a little higher, like a GBA), and a power light just for looks. When the window is taller than it is wide (a phone held upright), a card asks you to turn the phone and the game waits.
- A/B badges appear only on a card's bottom action buttons and in the menu, never on things you tap directly (answers, words in sentences). The A badge is always on a button's top-left corner and the B badge on its top-right, tucked in so they never overlap a neighbouring button, and the button gives it room so it never covers a letter (on a small phone, `data-screen` on the page, set by fit() in js/main.js, and the cards' container queries on #modal).
- **A card with more than it shows** has ▼ More by its buttons (`moreCue` in js/cards.js) until you reach its end; tapping it, or the D-pad's down, scrolls the card, and up scrolls back (on a card whose D-pad moves between buttons, the button you choose comes into sight instead). The labels under A and B never move the buttons: a long one (more than 10 letters, such as How it's made) wraps onto two lines (decided 25 October 2026: on one line it looked squashed), and a button's words longer than 13 letters give just the first (Copy for Copy to a file), unless it has its own `data-label`.
- Messages appear in a box at the bottom of the game screen (in a conversation, they wait in the speech box until A), or at the top straight after a speech box that was there, till you move or turn, so they don't jump from top to bottom (`messageTop` in js/draw.js). A new task is announced with a message, and a red dot stays on Tasks until you look at your tasks.
