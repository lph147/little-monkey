# 小猴子 Little Monkey — Game design

Last updated 18 October 2026

Little Monkey is a vocabulary-collecting adventure through *Journey to the West*: you catch Chinese words the way Pokémon players catch creatures, hear them again and again in simple sentences from the story's characters, and earn progress by practising.

## Vision

The game's job is to build a large, well-remembered vocabulary, with lots of repeated input of simple Chinese sentences, wrapped in a story worth following. It's a relaxed game: it never tests grammar. Sentence patterns are absorbed by hearing and reading them, not taught or tested. Reading the Jeff Pepper books is a longer-term benefit, not what the first areas are measured against.

It is not a translation tool, a flashcard app with a skin, or a game where walking around counts as learning. Every system should either put a word in front of you, ask you to recognise it, or ask you to use it.

**Four pillars**

| Pillar | What it means | Where you see it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collect | Catch words like creatures and complete a collection | Word Scroll, animals in the grass, new items in each area |
| Hear | Meet your words again and again in simple sentences, from the story's characters | Conversations, filling gaps in sentences, shops |
| Journey | Follow Sun Wukong's story from stone to pilgrim | Areas, quests, cutscenes |
| Grow | Learning pays for progress | The words you know, friends, shells and coins, shops, abilities |

## Core loop

Every area runs the same loop: you meet new words in the world, catch them, use them, train them, and the rewards unlock the next part of the journey.

Explore (a new area of the story) → Meet (grass, people, shops) → Catch (a new word card) → Use (quests, shops, sentences) → Train (the rolling word test) → Grow (friends, shells, abilities) → back to Explore. The review scheduler decides which words come back at each step.

Catching is the heart of it, and the review scheduler feeds old words back into every other step.

## The game's mechanics (the long-term plan)

Decided on 18 October 2026, so the first area can prepare you for what comes later, and agreed from a plan with mockups (the [game plan](https://claude.ai/artifact/5pJaFHNBeLDFVGUUntw9VK)). Every mechanic is about words, never grammar, and each has a gentle first version on Flower Fruit Mountain.

| Mechanic | Later in the game | First on Flower Fruit Mountain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explore with abilities | Like Pokémon's Cut and Surf: each ability is a word you learn and then use to open the map, such as 游 swim (Area 2), and 变成 transform and the cloud somersault (Area 3) | Rocks that roll away, 跳 jump through the waterfall, and your stick: it knocks down dry, dead pines and breaks cracked rocks (like Cut and Rock Smash), which can open a way, as the cracked rock in the pine wood does |
| Befriend, then battle | Battles with hearts on both sides (see Battles, below): wild creatures from the end of the prologue, demons from Area 3 | Animal encounters, food that helps (the apple), and the first battles on the path up the stream, with the stick the big monkey gives you |
| Build | Gather materials and build things for others: the raft that ends Area 1, and something in each area after | The troop's school: gather wood (木头) and stone to build it |
| Learn at school | Lessons review the words that are due; each area's word test is its exam | The school replaces the practice post (built 21 October 2026: the troop uses the post to build it), with the old monkey as teacher (老师); hide and seek happens there, once you know enough places, and practice battles with the big monkey |
| Grow | No levels: progress is the words you know (see Progression). A bigger collection means a bigger pool for every battle, game and lesson, and words you know better get harder questions | Shells, friends, and the Word Scroll filling up |

**How it ties together.** The school can't teach him how to live forever, so he leaves to find a real teacher (Area 3). Building the school leads to building the raft. Animal friends made from the first day turn up again further along, to play and give advice. The apple in encounters becomes food that brings back hearts. The big monkey's stick becomes the staff (金箍棒) in Area 4. 跳, learned at the waterfall, becomes the move that dodges. The cave's stone beds are where you rest. Peaches, the first fruit you pick, become the Peaches of Immortality that make you stronger in Heaven (Area 5).

**Guided by characters, not prompts** (decided 18 October 2026). Playtesters ignored the task prompts and tips, which stayed flashing for ages. What to do next comes from a character in the speech box, as part of the story: the old monkey is your guide and checks in after each step, and other monkeys mention where things are. The task list stays as a reminder, and its dot doesn't flash.

## The Word Scroll

The Word Scroll becomes the game's Pokédex: a collection you want to complete, split into categories, where every entry is a word you have met.

**Heard, then caught.** A word you have only heard in conversation shows as a silhouette marked "heard", with a count, until you catch it with its new-word card. Words not taught yet go in a Heard list at the end of the scroll, with their counts, and are caught where they're taught: 有, heard nine times in the prologue, is caught in chapter 1, in the Water Curtain Cave. This turns the grey "just listen" words into a pipeline instead of a dead end.

**Categories:** animals, food, nature, places, items, actions (verbs), and people and phrases. Each shows a completion count, such as "Animals 6/10". A category only appears once there are words in it (Flower Fruit Mountain has no items).

**Phrases** (你好, 谢谢, 再见 and so on) are a collection of their own, on a Phrases page. They don't count towards a chapter's words, and they earn their Used mastery when you reply with them (see Grammar through characters).

**Each entry holds:** its pixel sprite, the word with pinyin and sound, an example sentence from the game, where you found it, and a mastery level.

| Mastery | Earned by |
| --- | --- |
| Heard | Hearing it in a sentence |
| Caught | Its new-word card |
| Recognised | Picking it from sound alone, twice in a row |
| Read | Picking it from its written form (characters with pinyin), twice in a row |
| Used | Using it in a sentence, a shop order or a quest, once you can read it |

**Chinese only, once caught.** After the new-word card, the Chinese name is the only name shown in the world, with English a tap away. That is part of why Pokémon names stick: nobody thinks of them in translation.

**Word building blocks.** Entries show how compound words are built, such as 山羊 = 山 (mountain) + 羊 (sheep or goat), so earlier words make later ones easier to remember.

## Repetition

A spaced-repetition scheduler sits behind everything and decides which words come back, so repetition happens through play rather than drills.

**Target:** every caught word is met at least 8 times in its first week, through at least 3 different channels. These numbers are a starting point to tune by playtesting.

| Channel | How the scheduler uses it |
| --- | --- |
| Animals in the grass | Animals whose names need practice, or are near the front of the review list, appear more often; their questions come from the front of the list |
| Shops | Items near the front of the list are stocked and highlighted |
| Quests | Requests ("我要…") ask for words near the front of the list |
| Conversations | Characters' lines reuse caught words from earlier areas |
| Training ground | The rolling word test takes words from the front of the list |
| Transformations | Turning into a caught animal says and shows its name |

**The review list** (decided 20 October 2026: no days). Every word you've caught is in one list, worked through in turns: the game counts every question you answer. A new word goes in behind the first 3 words you got right last time, and a word you get wrong behind the first 2, so it comes back within a few questions, however many words are waiting. A word you get right goes back 4 questions, then 8, 16, 32 and at most 64, the more times in a row you've got it right, so words you know well come up less often and new and weak ones more. Questions pick at random from the first few words that suit them, so you can't tell what's coming. Because the count only goes up, every word's turn comes round: nothing gets stuck at the back (and a missed word only ever goes behind words you got right, so words you keep missing can't fill the front between them). A word **needs practice** until you've got it right twice in a row (new, or missed since): the practice post's !, its shells and a game's card count those. Words from earlier areas keep appearing in later ones, so nothing is learned once and then dropped. (Until 20 October 2026, gaps were 1, 3, 7 and 21 days; a missed word joined the back of the words due, and right answers in one sitting moved a word weeks ahead.)

## Sentences through characters

Each area's characters use one or two simple sentence patterns again and again, with words from your own collection: hear 我要… once, then with every food you've caught. Patterns are never taught as grammar or tested. The table is a guide to which patterns each area's sentences use, so they repeat and grow gradually.

**Sentence tasks fill a gap, never build a sentence.** Wherever a task involves a sentence (asking for something, buying, practising, some animal questions, the story's questions), you're given the whole sentence in Chinese, with pinyin, and in English, with one word missing, and you choose the missing word. The choices follow the word's level: its picture with the Chinese while the word is new, then Chinese and pinyin once you've recognised it. Choosing the word reads the whole sentence aloud. A wrong choice shows the right one and you carry on: it's practice, not a test.

| Area | Character | Patterns | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Flower Fruit Mountain, prologue | 老猴子 Old monkey and the troop | Phrases 你好, 再见, 谢谢, 不客气, 对不起, 没关系; 我要 / 我不要 + thing | 我要桃子。 |
| 1 Flower Fruit Mountain, chapter 1 | The troop, and you as their king | 这是…, 有 / 没有, numbers with 一只 / 一个 | 洞里有三只猴子。 |
| 2 Across the sea | Villagers and a market seller | Questions with 吗, 什么, 哪里; 不 | 这是什么？ |
| 3 The teacher's mountain | 菩提祖师 Master Subhuti | 会 / 能 (can), 想 (want to), 变成 (turn into) | 我会变成鸟。 |
| 4 Dragon King's palace | 龙王 the Dragon King | 在 + place, 给 (give), 几 / 多少 (how many) | 给我一个。 |
| 5 Heaven | 玉皇大帝 the Jade Emperor | 了 for finished actions, 很 / 太, 比 (comparisons) | 我吃了桃子。 |
| 6 Under the mountain | 唐僧 the monk | 因为…所以…, time words | 因为我很饿，所以我吃桃子。 |
| 7 onward | 猪八戒 the pig, 沙僧 Sandy | Longer sentences and story telling |  |

Patterns are met in characters' lines, gap sentences, shops and replies. The prologue's phrases were checked by a native speaker on 16 October 2026; the rest of this table still needs a check before it goes into the game.

**Replying.** Characters' lines invite a reply: you choose what to say from two or three phrases you've caught, each with pinyin, its sound and its English. In the first areas replies are input, not a test: the English is always shown (players who know some Chinese can turn it off in Settings). What you're answering stays in sight while you choose: the line just said (or what's happening, with a picture, such as a gift), put in plain English with what to do, such as "The hungry monkey thanks you. What do you say back?" (decided 22 October 2026: the line disappeared, and "Your reply" didn't say to what). Any choice can be made; one that doesn't fit is shown with why ("你好 (hello) is for when you meet someone. When someone thanks you, you say 不客气 (you're welcome).") and the one that does, which your monkey then says, so the conversation makes sense (it used to say goodbye to a thank you). A phrase's card says when you say it. 你好 starts a conversation and 再见 ends it, 谢谢 and 不客气 follow gifts and help (you say 谢谢 when the 老板 hands you what you've bought, and he says 不客气: he used to thank you, and you him, which made it a puzzle why a monkey's thanks wanted 不客气), and 对不起 and 没关系 follow mistakes. Because every conversation has them, the core phrases are heard and used dozens of times a session. The first time a phrase is needed it's taught on its own card, unless that conversation has already taught a new word: then it waits for the next time (your first request teaches 要 and your second 我, so 不客气 comes with a later one: the big monkey's thanks for the wood, in chapter 1; buying the headband teaches 头带, so 谢谢 comes when you buy something you know, or when the squirrel or the goat gives you fruit for winning its word game, where nothing else is new). 我不要 is taught in Area 1 as a fixed phrase; 不 on its own is still taught in Area 2.

## Story and areas

The game follows Sun Wukong's story in order, and stays true to it. Three words describe its shape:

- An **area** is a part of the world where a stretch of the story happens, such as Flower Fruit Mountain. You leave it when the story does, after a word test.
- A **chapter** is a part of the story: one for each place and part of the journey, so an area has one or two. Each brings new words (as many as the story needs: there's no fixed number, decided 20 October 2026), new requests and encounters, and reuses the words you already know.
- A **place** is one small map within an area. New places unlock as the story goes on, so you keep coming back to familiar ground with new things to do.

The school isn't in the book: it fills the gap where the story skips hundreds of years of happy life on the mountain, and sets up what comes next, as the school can't teach him how to live forever, so he goes to find a real teacher (Area 3). Nor are the hive, the garden or the cow: they're the troop's good life on the mountain, and the feast that life leads to is the one in the book.

**Prologue: the first day (built).** The baby is born from the stone. The old monkey, like a grandparent, welcomes it and sends it off to explore: you learn the names of things by finding them yourself (anything that sparkles has a word), and the monkeys you meet teach a few more through what they need: food, water, play, trading. The story is told through short lines, not long cutscenes. The waterfall is loud, and a little frightening; by the end of the day the troop wonders what's behind it. The prologue ends with the story of your first day, read at the waterfall.

**The end of the prologue: the Water Curtain Cave.** As in the book:

1. The troop gathers at the waterfall, and agrees that whoever can jump through and come back unhurt will be their king.
2. You jump (跳) through the water and find a cave behind it: an iron bridge and a stone home with stone beds (床), bowls (碗) and tables (桌子). (The bridge's word, 桥, waits for chapter 1, decided 21 October 2026: the cave comes just after 洞 and 瀑布, so three new words there are enough.) A stone tablet, two squares tall, has its name carved down it: 水帘洞 (Water Curtain Cave).
3. You jump back and lead the troop in. The cave becomes their home (家). As the troop moves in, you count three of them in (一 二 三: the old monkey counts them as each comes in, 一！ 一，二！ 一，二，三！, decided 21 October 2026, as 一只猴子。两只猴子。 taught 二 while saying 两; 两, for two of something, is a word of its own for later, such as 两个苹果 at the shop, and till then the number lessons never ask how many there are when there are two); four to ten wait for the school in chapter 1. The longer story from the book's opening, saved from the waterfall, is read here (see below).
4. **Chapter 1: the troop's new home, and the feast** (planned 20 October 2026). Inspired by Digimon World, where the town you come back to grows with each part of the story: the open ground beside the waterfall becomes the troop's settlement, 家园 (jiāyuán, Home ground: a place of its own, along a way the troop clears beside the waterfall), the base every new path leads out from and every quest brings something back to. *A: the troop's new home.*
- **Wood and stone** (built 21 October 2026). The big monkey, the troop's builder, wants wood and small stones for the school: 我们要木头和小石头！ First he marks out its plot beside him, as you watch: a stake hammered in at each corner, the string pulled tight round them, then the ground cleared, with its sign and the path up to it. He clears the bushes at the top of the home ground, and a path leads up to the pine wood (松林 sōnglín), the first new place in chapter 1. There, you knock down dry, dead pines with your stick, as with Cut and Rock Smash in Pokémon: a swing, chips flying, the tree comes down (a stump is left, for good), and the first one is where you find 木头 yourself. Small stones lie by the stream, to pick up. A cracked rock shuts a little clearing with the last dead pine and stone: one hit breaks it. You count each thing aloud in Chinese as you get it (一, 二, 三, the numbers you know, so it's three of each: later builds can ask for more once the school teaches 四 to 十). A squirrel there shows you the dry pines (its name, 松鼠, means pine mouse). Bring them back and the troop builds the school straight away (1. The school opens, below). No chopping (砍): the monkeys have no axe, and in the book Sun Wukong breaks off dead pines for his raft, so the raft's wood can come from the pine wood too (decided 21 October 2026, when a first plan of fetching things from places you'd already been was too dull).
- **The pine wood, laid out again, with a deer and wolves** (21 October 2026, after playing it on a phone twice: first it was small, with nothing else in it; then, made bigger, it made little sense, and you could walk in, pick everything up and never meet a thing). It's 34 by 30 squares, a maze of thick pines with ways two squares wide (a pine's top reaches up over the square above it, so a narrower way across would be hidden). In at the bottom, the way leads up to the squirrel's clearing, where the ways part: west to a hollow, east to a pool, and north through a meadow of long grass to the only bridge over the stream; over it, west along the stream and up into the oldest pines, or north-east up to a cracked rock, the only way into a sunny glade. There are three dry, dead pines and three small stones, one here and one there (the hollow, the pool's shore, by the stream, the oldest pines, and a dead pine and a stone in the glade), so you need every one and explore the whole wood; nothing hides behind the top of a pine. Every way to them goes through long grass, where wolves (狼 láng, three hearts, between a snake and the wild boar), snakes and spiders jump out: about 6 fights to collect everything, taking the shortest way (4 to 7 for most players, worked out with the game's own chances); you can always run away. The squirrel warns you, and says a 桃子 (peach) brings a heart back in a fight; lose one in chapter 1, and you wake up under the big tree at the home ground, not in the cave. The deer (鹿 lù) lives in the glade, a friend to make, chapter 1's animal to find, on the checklist, counted on its own so the prologue's animals, and the rocks that wait for them, are just as they were; the cracked rock can be broken any time in chapter 1, so no one is shut out of the glade. They're the wolves the cow will meet on its way home (3. The cow, below), so you'll know 狼 by then. 鹿 sounds just like 路 (lù, path), so a listening question never offers both. Saved games stay right: a square changed for good counts only while the map still has there what changed (no stump left in the middle of a new path), and if your saved spot is now among the pines, you start at the wood's way in. Its look and sound (built 21 October 2026): gloomy, but always in the daytime, never night (it mustn't look as if night has fallen there, with the home ground in sunshine; a first mock-up with moonlight was too like night): shade where the pines are thickest, lighter along the ways and in the clearings, sunbeams slanting into the open places, the glade in sunshine, mist drifting low, and now and then a pair of eyes glinting deep in the trees; crows, the wind in the pines and a far-off howl now and then, and a slow, quiet song of its own in a minor key. The first time you go in, a wolf howls far off: "It's gloomy under the thick pines, and very still." You arrive a few steps in, so a message at the bottom of the screen never hides you. Battles and the deer's questions there happen among its pines, with a battle song of its own, plainly not the usual one (decided 21 October 2026, when the first, the battle tune in a minor key, was too like it to notice): its own tune, slower, low on the xun and then on the dizi, over a drum like a heartbeat; a little tense, never frightening. Every note is one of the tune's five, and the wood's own song stops when it starts (in version 188 its long, low notes went on under the battle song, and the guzheng had notes outside the tune's: it sounded like two songs at once). The deer stands on the glade's grass, and hops where it stands when you meet it.

## Progression and economy

Money comes mainly from learning, so progress in the game and progress in Chinese move together.

**No XP or levels** (decided 18 October 2026). They had no effect on anything. Progress is the words you know: how many you've caught, and how well you know each one (the Word Scroll). That already makes the game grow with you, as a bigger collection is a bigger pool for every battle, game and lesson, and words you know better get harder questions. The menu and your tasks show your shells, words and friends. In battles you always start with the same hearts; help comes from friends and food, and a tougher fight means harder questions, not a bigger number. (XP and levels were added on 16 October 2026 and taken out two days later.)

**Money.** Shells are the money on Flower Fruit Mountain: the troop trades cowrie shells from the shore, as China did before coins (which is why 贝 is inside money words like 买, 卖 and 贵). From the human world (Area 2) onward, coins (钱) take over, which teaches the word naturally. (Peaches were the money at first, but you could pick them from the trees, so they became shells on 17 October 2026. Peaches are just food. 贝壳 isn't a word to catch for now.)

**Shops.** Every area has a shopkeeper who sells that area's items. Each item has a card with its picture, its Chinese with pinyin, its English and its price, so it's always clear what's for sale (decided 16 October 2026, replacing Chinese-only labels). Buying means filling the gap in a sentence, such as 我要＿ (I want an apple), with the word for what you want. Items are either useful (food that helps in encounters, tools such as a raft or rope) or cosmetic (decorations for your cave). Prices stay modest so the economy never becomes a grind. Until 一个 is taught in chapter 1, buying uses the prologue's pattern: 我要 + thing.

**Flower Fruit Mountain's shop.** A monkey shop owner, 老板 (lǎobǎn), sells a red cloth headband, 头带 (tóudài), for about 10 shells. It's something you choose to buy, not something you must do (decided 18 October 2026; it used to be on the prologue's checklist, and becoming king with the crown came straight after it). Your monkey wears it from then on. (The golden headband comes much later in the story, from the monk.)

**Fruit and the bag** (decided 18 October 2026). Each fruit does one thing, the same everywhere: a 桃子 (peach) brings a heart back (on your turn in a battle, with 吃, or from your bag), and a 苹果 (apple) takes away a wrong answer under any question, in a battle or with an animal, without using your turn. You carry up to 3 of each. Fruit trees fill your bag once the hungry monkey has eaten (one fruit a tree each time you come to the place), friends give fruit when you win their word games (the squirrel a peach, the goat an apple), and the 老板 sells apples. Later areas can add fruit with other jobs. Honey (蜂蜜, chapter 1) gives you energy: you walk faster for a while. The bag (包 bāo, on the menu) shows your food, how many and what each does, your stick, and what you're carrying for someone; tap a peach to eat it. Clothes stay in the Wardrobe.

**Wardrobe.** Clothes are a collection on a Wardrobe page (衣服): the items you own, each with its sprite, Chinese and pinyin. Putting one on says the sentence, such as 我戴头带。 or 我穿衣服。, which counts as using the words: 戴 is for things on your head, 穿 for clothes and shoes. The wardrobe starts on Flower Fruit Mountain with the 头带 headband, and opens fully in Area 2, where Sun Wukong takes human clothes to dress like a person (衣服, 帽子, 鞋). Items come from shops and request rewards, so each area's shop has clothes to sell as well as food and cave decorations.

**Training ground.** A place in every area where you can do a rolling word-matching test at any time. It takes its words from the front of the review list, so it always includes your newest words and the ones you've missed.

| Word in the test | Reward |
| --- | --- |
| Needs practice (new, or missed since, until right twice in a row), right first time | A shell |
| Known well | Nothing |
| Wrong answer | Nothing, and it comes back sooner |

This stops anyone farming easy words, and keeps the reward pointed at what actually needs practice. The troop's school will take over from the training ground on Flower Fruit Mountain (chapter 1 A).

**Moving on.** Each area ends with a word test covering that area's words, with an 80% pass mark, as part of the story: on Flower Fruit Mountain it's the troop's send-off on the shore, before you sail.

## Encounters and question types

Encounters are the game's battles: you win by answering, and every answer is a chance to meet a word again. Questions only ever use words you've already caught. An animal never asks about its own name, which is on the screen, and its picture is never one of the answers, so animals wait until you've caught 4 words with pictures. An encounter is a scene, like a Pokémon battle: your monkey, from behind, facing the animal on the grass (or its cliff), whose name plate has three hearts to fill and says which question this is. A right answer makes it hop and fills a heart; two hearts make a friend, with a new-friend moment at the end. Whichever answer you choose, the word is said and shown with its pinyin and English (Yes! 学校 xuéxiào · school), in case its picture wasn't clear (decided 21 October 2026; until then, the English came only with a wrong answer).

| Question | You get | You choose | Trains |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Listen | The word spoken | A picture, with its characters underneath | Sound to meaning |
| Read | The characters, with pinyin (never characters alone: decided 17 October 2026, as too hard for beginners) | A picture | Written word to meaning |
| Hear and read | The word spoken | Words in characters with pinyin | Sound to the written word |
| Fill the gap | A sentence in Chinese and English, one word missing | The missing word: a picture with its Chinese while it's new, then Chinese and pinyin | Words in context |
| Tones (later) | Two similar words spoken | Which one you heard | Tones |

When you only hear the word (Listen, Hear and read, and a battle's dodge), no two answers sound alike apart from their tones, such as 喝 hē and 河 hé: it's confusing, and a computer voice can't always make the tone clear (decided 21 October 2026). Reading, they can be answers together.

The question type follows the word's mastery level, so new words start easy and get harder as you know them better. A word needs two right answers in a row before its questions get harder, and each area says which kinds it asks: Flower Fruit Mountain asks only Listen and Read, so a beginner always has a picture to go on. Hear and read starts in a later area, for words you can already read. From Area 3, enemies (demons and guards) replace friendly animals in some places and ask harder questions, and abilities like transformations can be used in encounters. Battles are planned below.

### Battles

Agreed on 18 October 2026, with mockups. A battle looks like an animal encounter and plays like a Pokémon battle, but every hit or miss depends on whether you know the word: no levels, no luck, no timers.

- **A round.** Your turn: buttons in Chinese with pinyin and English, 打 (dǎ, attack), 吃 (chī, eat a peach, when you're hurt and have one) and 跑 (pǎo, run away; not from big enemies). Your move asks a question: right, you hit; wrong, you miss. Then the enemy's turn: it says where it's attacking from or what it throws (草！ 石头！ 火！) and you pick what you heard: right, you dodge; wrong, you lose a heart.
- **Moves come from the story**, and harder ones ask harder questions and hit harder: 打 with your stick (from the prologue; a Listen question, 1 heart), 跳 jump (the enemy's next attack misses), 变成 transform (Area 3: your next question loses a wrong answer), and the staff, 金箍棒 (Area 4: a Read question, 2 hearts; your stick becomes the staff).
- **No friend powers** (decided 18 October 2026, after playtesting): planned, then dropped to keep battles simple. Abilities like transforming (变成) come later, with the story.
- **Who you fight.** On Flower Fruit Mountain, wild creatures on the path up the stream: snakes and bees in the long grass, a wild boar blocking the last stretch, all chased away, never hurt. Demons from Area 3 (little demons, then the Demon of Havoc, 混世魔王), the Dragon King's guards in Area 4, Heaven's soldiers in Area 5.
- **Where.** Wild creatures jump out of long grass on paths, drawn like the meadow's tall grass, a shade darker; bushes, boulders and ledges mean you can't always go round it, and standing in it hides your feet, as in Pokémon. Which creature jumps out is chance, from those that live there, but never the same three times in a row, unless it's all that lives there (decided 22 October 2026: by chance, a spider jumped out six times in a row). The meadow's tall grass keeps its friendly animals. Big enemies (the boar, demons) stand on the map guarding the way.
- **Bosses** come in stages; between them the boss says a line and you answer by filling the gap in a sentence (你是谁？ … 我是美猴王！).
- **Winning and losing.** The first meeting with a kind of enemy teaches its name on a card. Winning gives shells or coins, sometimes an item. Losing isn't punished: you're carried back to rest with full hearts (in the meadow, or by a bed in the Water Curtain Cave once you've found it), and the words you missed come back sooner. Hearts come back by eating a peach, resting with the old monkey, or sleeping (睡觉) in a bed in the Water Curtain Cave, which a monkey just inside the entrance tells you about once the troop has moved in (decided 19 October 2026).
- **Getting harder without levels.** The path's creatures ask about words you've known a while, with pictures; later enemies use harder kinds of question (characters by sound, sentence gaps, tones), and bosses mix them all.

**Hearts.** You start with 5, and they carry over between battles. Only enemies cost hearts; animal encounters never do. Eating a peach brings one back (it counts as using the word). Resting refills them all: in the prologue the old monkey, waiting at the foot of the path; from chapter 1 the cave's stone beds; somewhere to stay in each later area. The story raises the most you can have, above all the Peaches of Immortality in Heaven (Area 5). Hearts first appear when the big monkey gives you the stick.

Choosing one opens its card: Continue, Copy to a file, or Delete (asked once more, with Keep it as the main button, and a copy kept in the browser); an empty one has New game or Load from a file. Each game keeps its own settings, and a new game starts with the sound and reading settings you're using. Your game from before is Game 1, unchanged. Saving, loading and starting again moved here from Settings.
- **The Word Scroll** is a grid of sprites, with shadows for words you haven't caught and category tabs across the top. Today's words is a filter in it.
- **Played sideways, like a Game Boy Advance** (decided 17 October 2026). There's one layout: the screen in the middle, always 15 columns by 10 rows (the GBA's shape, 3:2), as big as fits; the D-pad, Select and Start on the left with a power light; A and B on the right with a speaker. A tall screen on an upright phone looked strange, so held upright a card asks you to turn the phone, and the game waits (if the phone's rotation lock is on, it has to be switched off). Every place must be at least 15 by 10 (a test checks). Cards sit over the screen, so they only have its height: they use its width instead (pictures beside words, answers in a row), and text and pictures follow the screen's height (13 to 18px text; pinyin never under 10.5px, so its tone marks can be read), so the same layouts fit an iPhone, an Android phone and a computer. On a small phone (an iPhone, about 270px of screen, and smaller) cards and the speech box are a little tighter, so the main thing on each fits and you stay in sight. It can be installed as an app (manifest.webmanifest, sw.js) to get the whole screen: on Android it opens full screen and stays sideways; on an iPhone it opens without Safari's bars, but the phone must still be turned.

## Technical approach

The current game is one hand-built map in one file. Adding areas that way would become unmanageable, so the engine is split from the content first.

- **Data-driven areas.** Each area is a block of data: its map, words, characters, lines, quests, shop stock and exit test. The engine reads it, so adding an area is writing content, not new code.
- **A shared word bank.** Every word is defined once, with its pinyin, meaning, category, sprite and the area it belongs to. The scheduler, the Word Scroll, shops and encounters all read from it.
- **Pixel pictures for every collectible.** Emoji look different on every device and clash with the pixel style, so animals, food and items get their own pixel pictures. They're 32 × 32 (decided 17 October 2026: at 16 × 16 they looked too rough on cards, and 你好 (hello) was hard to read), drawn from shapes in tools/draw-pictures.js, which shades and outlines them, so a new one is quick to make. They're shown at sizes that give each pixel a whole number of the screen's real pixels, so they stay sharp. The animals walking on the map stay 16 × 16, a square's size. This is the biggest cost per area.
- **Saving.** Progress stays in the browser, plus a save export and import, so a cleared browser doesn't lose weeks of play.
- **Audio.** Voice clips made in advance (since 19 October 2026; before, the device's Chinese voice). The plan (19 October 2026): pick a computer voice from Azure with a listening test (tools/try-voices.html), then make an audio file for every word and line in advance, so every device sounds the same and works offline, with the device's voice for anything without a file. Recorded native audio replaces the files later, under the same names, once the game is finished. **Chosen after the listening test (19 October 2026): Yunxi (zh-CN-YunxiNeural, a young man) for the words and most lines, and Yunze (zh-CN-YunzeNeural, an older man) for the old monkey.** **Changed after playing it on a Pixel 8 Pro (19 October 2026):** slowed down by 15%, with silence and breaths around it, Yunxi sounded slow and exaggerated. The second listening test chose Yunxi at normal speed, tidy (no silence before or after, short pauses between sentences) and in its relaxed chat style for sentences, and showed that giving a voice the pinyin fixes a word it reads wrongly (只). A native speaker agreed that single words sound clearer in a woman's voice, so words said on their own (new-word cards, tapping a word, listening questions) get their own voice: Xiaoxiao (zh-CN-XiaoxiaoNeural), chosen in a third test. In the game some single syllables (水, 草, 喝) still sounded strange in every Xiaoxiao setting, while the Pixel's own (Google's) voice said them well, so a fifth test chose Google's Standard A (cmn-CN-Standard-A) for words (20 October 2026); its clips are made on GitHub, which keeps Google's key. Lines keep Yunxi, and the old monkey Yunze, tidied the same way.
- **Drawing the map.** A place's map is drawn once when you go there (its ground, and the big objects that never move, into layers under and over the characters); when the story changes a few squares (a dead pine knocked down), only they are drawn again, with what's around them (decided 22 October 2026: drawing the whole pine wood again took two seconds on a phone). Each frame then just copies the part you see.
- **Format.** A web game that runs in a phone browser, hosted on GitHub Pages and built in Claude Code.

## Risks and open questions

| Risk | What could go wrong | Plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | A full RPG is a lot to build | Data-driven areas, built a chapter at a time; finish Area 1 well before adding Area 2 |
| A long first area | Area 1 has two chapters, about 50 words | Chapter 1 mostly reuses the prologue's words, and new places keep it fresh; playtest the prologue first |
| Art | Every collectible needs a sprite | Reuse palettes and shapes; aim for 15–20 new sprites per area |
| Time per session | Walking and menus crowd out learning | Target at least half of play time hearing, reading or answering |
| Reward farming | Players repeat easy words for rewards | Shells only for words that need practice, as above |
| Chinese accuracy | Unnatural lines as grammar gets harder | Native-speaker review of each area's lines |
| Lost progress | A cleared browser wipes the save | Save export and import |

**Decided on 16 October 2026: a vocabulary game, never a grammar test.** Sentence tasks always give the whole sentence in Chinese and English with one word missing, and you choose the word; word tiles in order are gone. The choices follow the word's level (pictures while new, then Chinese and pinyin). This makes the game more relaxed while still giving lots of repeated input of simple sentence structures.

**Decided after playtesting stage 2** (16 October 2026): Flower Fruit Mountain must suit someone who speaks no Chinese yet, so they can spend longer there getting to know its words.

- **No emoji** (decided 18 October 2026): every icon is pixel art like the rest of the game (a star, a scroll, a clipboard, a paw, a shirt, a cog, a floppy disk, a book, a cup, mountains, the shell, the speaker and the heart), drawn in tools/draw-pictures.js at 16 × 16. The text still says them as emoji, and the game shows the pixel icon instead.
- **Writing in the world is real Chinese** (decided 21 October 2026, when the school's sign had made-up squiggles on it): a sign, a tablet or a blackboard says something real, in characters drawn by hand in pixels, so there's always more to read: 学校 on the school's sign, 水帘洞 carved down the cave's stone tablet, the numbers 一 to 十 on the blackboard. Words from the word bank where it can be, or a name. Word pictures (a question's answers) have no writing in them: a word written in one could give an answer away (三 on the teacher's blackboard, in a question about 三).
- **The question layout** (decided 18 October 2026): a question starts at the top, with the speaker beside "Listen. Which picture is it?", and a bottom row whose space is always kept: what happened ("Yes! 山 shān") on the left and Next on the right, so nothing moves when you answer. When listening, the answers show their characters, and their pinyin once you've answered. Food from the shop is a small hint in that bottom row ("Eat a 苹果 (apple): one wrong answer goes"), in plain text, not a big button. The pixel font is kept for short English labels and buttons; Chinese and sentences are never in it.
- **Questions get harder slowly:** two right answers in a row at each step, and no picking characters by sound (without pictures) on Flower Fruit Mountain.
- **Building a sentence** marks its words as used, but doesn't skip the questions: a word is Used once you can read it too.
- **Every word you practise has a picture,** including 瀑布, 老板 and 头带. 我 and 要 are practised by building 我要 sentences at the practice post.
- **The story at the waterfall** is a short one made of sentences you've already met, each with a picture, and its questions fill a gap with a picture. The longer story from the book's opening moves to the Water Curtain Cave, which teaches 有, 一只 and 一个.
