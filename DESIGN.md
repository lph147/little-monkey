# 小猴子 Little Monkey — Game design

Last updated 27 October 2026

Little Monkey is a vocabulary-collecting adventure through *Journey to the West*: you catch Chinese words the way Pokémon players catch creatures, hear them again and again in simple sentences from the story's characters, and earn progress by practising.

## Vision

The game's job is to build a large, well-remembered vocabulary, with lots of repeated input of simple Chinese sentences, wrapped in a story worth following. It's a relaxed game: it never tests grammar. Sentence patterns are absorbed by hearing and reading them, not taught or tested. Reading the Jeff Pepper books is a longer-term benefit, not what the first areas are measured against.

It is not a translation tool, a flashcard app with a skin, or a game where walking around counts as learning. Every system should either put a word in front of you, ask you to recognise it, or ask you to use it.

**Four pillars**

| Pillar | What it means | Where you see it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collect | Catch words like creatures and complete a collection | Word Scroll, animals in the grass, new items in each area |
| Hear | Meet your words again and again in simple sentences, from the story's characters | Conversations, filling gaps in sentences, shops |
| Journey | Follow Sun Wukong's story from stone to pilgrim | Areas, quests, cutscenes |
| Grow | Learning pays for progress | The words you know, friends, shells and coins, shops, abilities |

## Core loop

Every area runs the same loop: you meet new words in the world, catch them, use them, train them, and the rewards unlock the next part of the journey.

Explore (a new area of the story) → Meet (grass, people, shops) → Catch (a new word card) → Use (quests, shops, sentences) → Train (the rolling word test) → Grow (friends, shells, abilities) → back to Explore. The review scheduler decides which words come back at each step.

Catching is the heart of it, and the review scheduler feeds old words back into every other step.

## The game's mechanics (the long-term plan)

Decided on 18 October 2026, so the first area can prepare you for what comes later, and agreed from a plan with mockups (the [game plan](https://claude.ai/artifact/5pJaFHNBeLDFVGUUntw9VK)). Every mechanic is about words, never grammar, and each has a gentle first version on Flower Fruit Mountain.

| Mechanic | Later in the game | First on Flower Fruit Mountain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explore with abilities | Like Pokémon's Cut and Surf: each ability is a word you learn and then use to open the map, such as 游 swim, and 变成 transform and the cloud somersault (all in Area 2) | Rocks that roll away, 跳 jump through the waterfall, and your stick: it knocks down dry, dead pines and breaks cracked rocks (like Cut and Rock Smash), which can open a way, as the cracked rock in the pine wood does |
| Befriend, then battle | Battles with health on both sides (see Battles, below): wild creatures from the end of the prologue, demons from the end of Area 2 | Animal encounters, food that helps (the apple), and the first battles on the path up the stream, with the stick the big monkey gives you |
| Build | Gather materials and build things for others: the raft that ends Area 1, and something in each area after | The troop's school: gather wood (木头) and stone to build it |
| Learn at school | Lessons review the words that are due; each area's word test is its exam | The school replaces the practice post (built 21 October 2026: the troop uses the post to build it), with the old monkey as teacher (老师); hide and seek happens there, once you know enough places, and practice battles with the big monkey |
| Grow | No levels: progress is the words you know (see Progression). A bigger collection means a bigger pool for every battle, game and lesson, and words you know better get harder questions | Shells, friends, and the Word Scroll filling up |

**How it ties together.** The school can't teach him how to live forever, so he leaves to find a real teacher (Area 2). Building the school leads to building the raft. Animal friends made from the first day turn up again further along, to play and give advice. The apple in encounters becomes food that brings back health. The big monkey's stick becomes the staff (金箍棒) in Area 3. 跳, learned at the waterfall, becomes the move that dodges. The cave's stone beds are where you rest. Peaches, the first fruit you pick, become the Peaches of Immortality that make you stronger in Heaven (Area 4).

**Guided by characters, not prompts** (decided 18 October 2026). Playtesters ignored the task prompts and tips, which stayed flashing for ages. What to do next comes from a character in the speech box, as part of the story: the old monkey is your guide and checks in after each step, and other monkeys mention where things are. The task list stays as a reminder, and its dot doesn't flash.

## The Word Scroll

The Word Scroll becomes the game's Pokédex: a collection you want to complete, split into categories, where every entry is a word you have met.

**Heard, then caught.** A word you have only heard in conversation shows as a silhouette marked "heard", with a count, until you catch it with its new-word card. Words not taught yet go in a Heard list at the end of the scroll, with their counts, and are caught where they're taught: 有, heard nine times in the prologue, is caught in chapter 1, in the Water Curtain Cave. This turns the grey "just listen" words into a pipeline instead of a dead end.

**Categories:** animals, food, nature, places, items, actions (verbs), and people and phrases. Each shows a completion count, such as "Animals 6/10". A category only appears once there are words in it (Flower Fruit Mountain has no items).

**Phrases** (你好, 谢谢, 再见 and so on) are a collection of their own, on a Phrases page. They don't count towards a chapter's words, and they earn their Used mastery when you reply with them (see Grammar through characters).

**Each entry holds:** its pixel sprite, the word with pinyin and sound, an example sentence from the game, where you found it, and a mastery level.

| Mastery | Earned by |
| --- | --- |
| Heard | Hearing it in a sentence |
| Caught | Its new-word card |
| Recognised | Picking it from sound alone, twice in a row |
| Read | Picking it from its written form (characters with pinyin), twice in a row |
| Used | Using it in a sentence, a shop order or a quest, once you can read it |

**Chinese only, once caught.** After the new-word card, the Chinese name is the only name shown in the world, with English a tap away. That is part of why Pokémon names stick: nobody thinks of them in translation.

**Word building blocks.** Entries show how compound words are built, such as 山羊 = 山 (mountain) + 羊 (sheep or goat), so earlier words make later ones easier to remember.

**Chinese is logical, from the first day** (decided 23 October 2026). The prologue shows how easy and logical Chinese can be: you learn short words, then find them inside longer ones, and by the end you can read the name of your home. In the meadow: 山 (mountain, the cliffs); 羊 (sheep), a sheep eating grass at the foot of the cliffs near your stone, which took the fox's place; then the goat on its rock to the south, 山羊 = 山 + 羊, a mountain sheep. 水 (water, for the thirsty monkey); 水果 (fruit), which the hungry monkey teaches as he asks for some (我饿了。我要吃水果！); each fruit tree teaches its own fruit, as you look at it or pick one for him, sparkling till you know it: 桃子 (peach), and 苹果 (apple), which shares its 果 with 水果. (Until 24 October 2026 every fruit tree showed you 水果 first, sparkling once you knew 水, and picking taught its fruit; the game's owner asked for each tree to say its own fruit, and the hungry monkey to teach 水果. So 吃, which he taught as he asked, comes when you give him the fruit he wants, just before you finish 我要吃…, and 我, which that took the place of, is the big monkey's in the meadow, as he says who he is: 你好！我是大猴子。 A game that helped the hungry monkey without learning 水果 gets a ! over him, and he teaches it.) And once you know 花, 山 and 水果, the sign by your stone sparkles: 花果山 = 花 (flower) + 果 (fruit) + 山 (mountain), named after what grows there, which you find out by tapping each character in turn (since 24 October 2026). The goat gives the tip that each character is one syllable (shān + yáng: two syllables, two characters), which you try on a drum, hearing each beat as its character lights up. A link shows on whichever of two words you meet second, so the order is never forced; the map and the sparkles lead most players through it in this order. The fox comes in chapter 1, hiding in the hillside's tall grass. Built in four steps: the sheep and the fruit; how a word is made, on its card and in the Word Scroll, with the links to words you know; the name of your home and the goat's tip; then the rest of the words whose parts genuinely help (see Next steps).

**How a word is made** (built 23 October 2026, from a plan and a mock-up). With a new word's card, a short story says how the word is made, in one of three ways: its parts, each with its pinyin over it and its English, and the word they make, with its own English, what it means (山 mountain + 羊 sheep = 山羊 goat; until 25 October 2026 it said what the parts add up to, a mountain sheep, which the game's owner saw read as if that were what 山羊 means); the clue inside a character (口 mouth, on the left of 吃: you eat with your mouth); or the picture a character was drawn from (山: drawn like three mountain peaks). A part you know as a word is green, the rest grey, and every part has its pinyin. Under it, one line says where you've met a part before, in another word you know (果 is also in 水果, a word you know), whichever of the two you meet second. It goes with the English: since 24 October 2026 on a card of its own (the box crowded the word's card), straight after a new word's first card (How it's made ▸, then Got it; B goes back to the word), and after that from the English button, which shows the English and then becomes How it's made, on a word's card and its page in the Word Scroll alike. On its card, each part is a column: its picture, if it's a word with one (山, 羊, 水), its pinyin, its character and its English, lined up so the characters are in a row, then = the word, with its English; a clue shows the character big with the clue beside it, and a picture what the character was drawn from, beside it. A word made of parts comes together as you watch (step 3): the parts come in one by one, then each flies into the word, whose picture and meaning come last; a part you've met inside another word you know shows that word first, with its picture, and lifts out of it (as in 水果). The parts aren't new words: nothing extra is caught or asked about, so one new word at a time still holds, and they aren't said on their own (a character alone can sound different: 子 is zǐ alone, zi in 猴子), so 🔊 still says the whole word. On the smallest phones, its card has smaller pictures and characters, so it all fits without scrolling. Only where it genuinely helps you understand or remember a word: never for characters that just say the meaning again (朋友, 睡觉, 谢谢), never a story that isn't true, and nothing at all for a word with no clear story. A part can name a word with it that has no box of its own, so its card can still say where you've met it (the 学 of 同学 is also in 学校; the 不 of 我不要, in 不客气). Since 24 October 2026, after playing it, only what's simple, useful and memorable: you get it in one look, with no history lesson, and every part is something you can picture, a word or a shape that looks like what it means (the 艹 of 花 and 草, plants, meant nothing to a beginner). So the pictures (山 水 鱼 羊 一 二 三), the parts that obviously add up (山羊, 动物, 松鼠, 头带, 野猪, 蜜蜂, 同学, 你好, just hello, 我不要), and the clues you can see (木, drawn like a tree, in 树 and 木头; 口, a mouth, in 吃 and 喝); and nothing for 河 (氵, three drops of water, wasn't much help: dropped 25 October 2026), 再见 (again + to see, no help either: dropped 26 October 2026), 花, 草, 桥, 床, 路, 跳, 碗, 家, 瀑布, 没关系 or 不客气. A word with nothing simpler, whose other character means nothing on its own, shows the character it shares with a word you know instead, once you know it: 苹果 and 水果 side by side, the 果 they share lit in each (Both have 果 guǒ (fruit): an apple is a fruit.); before you know 水果, 苹果 has none (水果 itself has no How it's made since 25 October 2026: water + fruit = juicy fruit wasn't much help). The explanations are in the word bank (MADE, content/words.js), and in CHINESE.md for the native speaker's check. The meadow's 20 words first, then the rest of Flower Fruit Mountain's 15 (step 4).

## Repetition

A spaced-repetition scheduler sits behind everything and decides which words come back, so repetition happens through play rather than drills.

**Target:** every caught word is met at least 8 times in its first week, through at least 3 different channels. These numbers are a starting point to tune by playtesting.

| Channel | How the scheduler uses it |
| --- | --- |
| Animals in the grass | Animals whose names need practice, or are near the front of the review list, appear more often; their questions come from the front of the list |
| Shops | Items near the front of the list are stocked and highlighted |
| Quests | Requests ("我要…") ask for words near the front of the list |
| Conversations | Characters' lines reuse caught words from earlier areas |
| Training ground | The rolling word test takes words from the front of the list |
| Transformations | Turning into a caught animal says and shows its name |

**The review list** (decided 20 October 2026: no days). Every word you've caught is in one list, worked through in turns: the game counts every question you answer. A new word goes in behind the first 3 words you got right last time, and a word you get wrong behind the first 2, so it comes back within a few questions, however many words are waiting. A word you get right goes back 4 questions, then 8, 16, 32 and at most 64, the more times in a row you've got it right, so words you know well come up less often and new and weak ones more. Questions pick at random from the first few words that suit them, so you can't tell what's coming. Because the count only goes up, every word's turn comes round: nothing gets stuck at the back (and a missed word only ever goes behind words you got right, so words you keep missing can't fill the front between them). A word **needs practice** until you've got it right twice in a row (new, or missed since): the practice post's !, its shells and a game's card count those. Words from earlier areas keep appearing in later ones, so nothing is learned once and then dropped. (Until 20 October 2026, gaps were 1, 3, 7 and 21 days; a missed word joined the back of the words due, and right answers in one sitting moved a word weeks ahead.)

## Sentences through characters

Each area's characters use one or two simple sentence patterns again and again, with words from your own collection: hear 我要… once, then with every food you've caught. Patterns are never taught as grammar or tested. The table is a guide to which patterns each area's sentences use, so they repeat and grow gradually.

**Sentence tasks fill a gap, never build a sentence.** Wherever a task involves a sentence (asking for something, buying, practising, some animal questions, the story's questions), you're given the whole sentence in Chinese, with pinyin, and in English, with one word missing, and you choose the missing word. The choices follow the word's level: its picture with the Chinese while the word is new, then Chinese and pinyin once you've recognised it. Choosing the word reads the whole sentence aloud. A wrong choice shows the right one and you carry on: it's practice, not a test. The one exception is the shop (decided 29 October 2026, after playing it: he handed over what you'd pointed at, though you'd asked for something else): ask for something you didn't point at, and the 老板 is puzzled, a ? over him (The 老板 looks puzzled: you pointed at the 苹果 (apple), but asked for something else! Ask him again.), and asks again, 你要什么？; the same choices come again, the right one somewhere else, so you find it rather than tap where it was, till you've asked for it, and only then do you pay. You can't get stuck, as the right one is shown each time, and asking again counts for nothing, as you've just seen the answer.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Flower Fruit Mountain, prologue | 老猴子 Old monkey and the troop | Phrases 你好, 再见, 谢谢, 不客气, 对不起, 没关系; 我要 / 我不要 + thing | 我要桃子。 |
| 1 Flower Fruit Mountain, chapter 1 | The troop, and you as their king | 这是…, 有 / 没有, numbers with 一只 / 一个 | 洞里有三只猴子。 |
| 2 Across the sea, prologue | Villagers and a market seller | Questions with 吗, 什么, 哪里; 不 | 这是什么？ |
| 2 Across the sea, chapter 1 | 菩提祖师 Master Subhuti | 会 / 能 (can), 想 (want to), 变成 (turn into) | 我会变成鸟。 |
| 3 Dragon King's palace | 龙王 the Dragon King | 在 + place, 给 (give), 几 / 多少 (how many) | 给我一个。 |
| 4 Heaven | 玉皇大帝 the Jade Emperor | 了 for finished actions, 很 / 太, 比 (comparisons) | 我吃了桃子。 |
| 5 Under the mountain | 唐僧 the monk | 因为…所以…, time words | 因为我很饿，所以我吃桃子。 |

Patterns are met in characters' lines, gap sentences, shops and replies. The prologue's phrases were checked by a native speaker on 16 October 2026; the rest of this table still needs a check before it goes into the game.

**Replying.** Characters' lines invite a reply: you choose what to say from two or three phrases you've caught, each with pinyin, its sound and its English. In the first areas replies are input, not a test: the English is always shown (players who know some Chinese can turn it off in Settings). What you're answering stays in sight while you choose: the line just said (or what's happening, with a picture, such as a gift), put in plain English with what to do, such as "The hungry monkey thanks you. What do you say back?" (decided 22 October 2026: the line disappeared, and "Your reply" didn't say to what). Any choice can be made; one that doesn't fit is shown with why ("你好 (hello) is for when you meet someone. When someone thanks you, you say 不客气 (you're welcome).") and the one that does, which your monkey then says, so the conversation makes sense (it used to say goodbye to a thank you). A phrase's card says when you say it. 你好 starts a conversation and 再见 ends it, 谢谢 and 不客气 follow gifts and help (you say 谢谢 when the 老板 hands you what you've bought, and he says 不客气: he used to thank you, and you him, which made it a puzzle why a monkey's thanks wanted 不客气), and 对不起 and 没关系 follow mistakes. Because every conversation has them, the core phrases are heard and used dozens of times a session. The first time a phrase is needed it's taught on its own card, unless that conversation has already taught a new word: then it waits for the next time (your first request teaches 要 and your second 我, so 不客气 comes with a later one: the big monkey's thanks for the flowers, in chapter 1, since his thanks for the wood and stones come just before the troop builds the school, teaching 学校, and want no reply; buying the headband teaches 头带, so 谢谢 comes when you buy something you know, or when the squirrel or the goat gives you fruit for winning its word game, where nothing else is new). 我不要 is taught in Area 1 as a fixed phrase; 不 on its own is still taught in Area 2.

## Story and areas

The game follows Sun Wukong's story in order, and stays true to it. Three words describe its shape:

- An **area** is a part of the world where a stretch of the story happens, such as Flower Fruit Mountain. You leave it when the story does, after a word test.
- A **chapter** is a part of the story: one for each place and part of the journey, so an area has one or two. Each brings new words (as many as the story needs: there's no fixed number, decided 20 October 2026), new requests and encounters, and reuses the words you already know.
- A **place** is one small map within an area. New places unlock as the story goes on, so you keep coming back to familiar ground with new things to do.

The school isn't in the book: it fills the gap where the story skips hundreds of years of happy life on the mountain, and sets up what comes next, as the school can't teach him how to live forever, so he goes to find a real teacher (Area 2). Nor are the hive, the garden or the cow: they're the troop's good life on the mountain, and the feast that life leads to is the one in the book.

**Prologue: the first day (built).** The baby is born from the stone. The old monkey, like a grandparent, welcomes it and sends it off to explore: you learn the names of things by finding them yourself (anything that sparkles has a word: for your first 4, the sparkles are bigger and the task's arrow shows you the nearest), and the monkeys you meet teach a few more through what they need: food, water, play, trading. The story is told through short lines, not long cutscenes. The waterfall is loud, and a little frightening; by the end of the day the troop wonders what's behind it. The prologue ends with the story of your first day, read at the waterfall.

## Progression and economy

Money comes mainly from learning, so progress in the game and progress in Chinese move together.

**No XP or levels** (decided 18 October 2026). They had no effect on anything. Progress is the words you know: how many you've caught, and how well you know each one (the Word Scroll). That already makes the game grow with you, as a bigger collection is a bigger pool for every battle, game and lesson, and words you know better get harder questions. The menu and your tasks show your shells, words and friends. In battles you always start with the same health; help comes from friends and food, and a tougher fight means harder questions, not a bigger number (health is a number, but a fight is set by how many right answers it takes: see Health, below). (XP and levels were added on 16 October 2026 and taken out two days later.)

**Money.** Shells are the money on Flower Fruit Mountain: the troop trades cowrie shells from the shore, as China did before coins (which is why 贝 is inside money words like 买, 卖 and 贵). From the human world (Area 2) onward, coins (钱) take over, which teaches the word naturally. (Peaches were the money at first, but you could pick them from the trees, so they became shells on 17 October 2026. Peaches are just food. 贝壳 isn't a word to catch for now.)

**Shops.** Every area has a shopkeeper who sells that area's items. Each item has a card with its picture, its Chinese with pinyin, its English and its price, so it's always clear what's for sale (decided 16 October 2026, replacing Chinese-only labels). Buying means filling the gap in a sentence, such as 我要＿ (I want an apple), with the word for what you want. Items are either useful (food that helps in encounters, tools such as a raft or rope) or cosmetic (decorations for your cave). Prices stay modest so the economy never becomes a grind. Until 一个 is taught in chapter 1, buying uses the prologue's pattern: 我要 + thing.

**Flower Fruit Mountain's shop.** A monkey shop owner, 老板 (lǎobǎn), sells a red cloth headband, 头带 (tóudài), for about 10 shells. It's something you choose to buy, not something you must do (decided 18 October 2026; it used to be on the prologue's checklist, and becoming king with the crown came straight after it). Your monkey wears it from then on. In chapter 1 he moves up to the home ground, with headbands in every colour you know (see the new shop). (The golden headband comes much later in the story, from the monk.)

**Fruit and the bag** (decided 18 October 2026). Each fruit does one thing, the same everywhere: a 桃子 (peach) brings back 10 health (on your turn in a battle, with 吃, or from your bag), and a 苹果 (apple) takes away a wrong answer under any question, in a battle or with an animal, without using your turn. You carry up to 3 of each. Fruit trees fill your bag once the hungry monkey has eaten (one fruit a tree each time you come to the place), friends give fruit the first time you win their word games (the squirrel a peach, the goat an apple; then their games are just for practice, earning nothing, decided 24 October 2026, as with the little monkeys in training), and the 老板 sells apples. A friend whose fruit your bag has no room for offers it and keeps it for you, and your thanks waits until you come back with room, when it has a ! for you (decided 24 October 2026: it said your bag was full, then that it had given you the fruit, and you thanked it). Later areas can add fruit with other jobs. Honey (蜂蜜, chapter 1) gives you energy: you walk faster for a minute (built 25 October 2026: steps take 0.6 of the time, with golden sparks behind you, and a message when it wears off); the bees' hive fills your bag with it, one each time you come to the home ground, 2 at most. The bag (包 bāo, on the menu, or BAG) is in tabs (decided 27 October 2026, so it stays tidy as it fills up; headings on one long page were tried first): Food, how many of each and what it does; For someone, what you're carrying and who for (The thirsty monkey); and To fight with, your stick and how hard it hits (Hits for 10). Only tabs with something in them show, with no row of tabs for just one; it opens on Food every time, as that's what you open it for, so eating a peach takes no more presses than before; left and right on the D-pad switch tabs, as in the Word Scroll; and the tabs sit at the top of the card, so a tab with fewer things never moves them. Tap a peach or honey to eat it. Later areas can add a tab, for other kinds of thing. Clothes stay in the Wardrobe.

**Wardrobe.** Clothes are a collection on a Wardrobe page (衣服): the items you own, each with its sprite, Chinese and pinyin. Putting one on says the sentence, such as 我戴头带。 or 我穿衣服。, which counts as using the words: 戴 is for things on your head, 穿 for clothes and shoes. The wardrobe starts on Flower Fruit Mountain with the 头带 headband, and opens fully in Area 2, where Sun Wukong takes human clothes to dress like a person (衣服, 帽子, 鞋). Items come from shops and request rewards, so each area's shop has clothes to sell as well as food and cave decorations.

**Training ground.** A place in every area where you can do a rolling word-matching test at any time. It takes its words from the front of the review list, so it always includes your newest words and the ones you've missed.

| Word in the test | Reward |
| --- | --- |
| Needs practice (new, or missed since, until right twice in a row), right first time | A shell |
| Known well | Nothing |
| Wrong answer | Nothing, and it comes back sooner |

This stops anyone farming easy words, and keeps the reward pointed at what actually needs practice. The troop's school will take over from the training ground on Flower Fruit Mountain (chapter 1 A).

**Moving on.** Each area ends with a word test covering that area's words, with an 80% pass mark, as part of the story: on Flower Fruit Mountain it's the school's exam, before the feast (decided 29 October 2026: it was to be the troop's send-off on the shore, before you sail, but a test there would spoil the goodbye; the exam fits the school, and passing it is what lets the feast begin).

## Encounters and question types

Encounters are the game's battles: you win by answering, and every answer is a chance to meet a word again. Questions only ever use words you've already caught. An animal never asks about its own name, which is on the screen, and its picture is never one of the answers, so animals wait until you've caught 4 words with pictures. Before its questions, an animal says 我们玩吧！ (Let's play!) in the speech box, over its name in Chinese and pinyin, with what's happening under it in plain English (It wants to play! Answer its questions to make friends., or for a friend, Your 朋友 (friend) wants to play again!), and Play starts the questions; the first time you meet one, its new-word card says what's happening, then the line (decided 23 October 2026, after playing it: the questions just started, with nothing to say why). An encounter is a scene, like a Pokémon battle: your monkey, from behind, facing the animal on the grass (or its cliff), whose name plate has three hearts to fill and says which question this is. A right answer makes it hop and fills a heart; two hearts make a friend, with a new-friend moment at the end. Whichever answer you choose, the word is said and shown with its pinyin and English (Yes! 学校 xuéxiào · school), in case its picture wasn't clear (decided 21 October 2026; until then, the English came only with a wrong answer).

| Question | You get | You choose | Trains |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Listen | The word spoken | A picture, with its characters underneath | Sound to meaning |
| Read | The characters, with pinyin (never characters alone: decided 17 October 2026, as too hard for beginners) | A picture | Written word to meaning |
| Hear and read | The word spoken | Words in characters with pinyin | Sound to the written word |
| Fill the gap | A sentence in Chinese and English, one word missing | The missing word: a picture with its Chinese while it's new, then Chinese and pinyin | Words in context |
| Tones (later) | Two similar words spoken | Which one you heard | Tones |

When you only hear the word (Listen, Hear and read, and a battle's dodge), no two answers sound alike apart from their tones, such as 喝 hē and 河 hé: it's confusing, and a computer voice can't always make the tone clear (decided 21 October 2026). Reading, they can be answers together.

Two answers are never look-alikes, in any kind of question: words whose pictures share their main thing, such as 床 (bed) and 睡觉 (to sleep), both a bed, or whose meanings overlap, such as 家 (home) and 学校 (school), both houses, or 动物 (animal) and any animal. Matching words to pictures (the practice post, the old monkey's 我们看看！ and the word test), look-alikes are in different rounds whenever they can be. The word bank lists each word's look-alikes (`alike`), and a new word with a picture lists its own when it's added (decided 23 October 2026, after playing it: 学校 and 家 side by side were confusing, and more words would make it worse). The numbers and the colours aren't look-alikes of each other: telling them apart is what their questions are for.

The question type follows the word's mastery level, so new words start easy and get harder as you know them better. A word needs two right answers in a row before its questions get harder, and each area says which kinds it asks: Flower Fruit Mountain asks only Listen and Read, so a beginner always has a picture to go on. Hear and read starts in a later area, for words you can already read. From the end of Area 2, enemies (demons and guards) replace friendly animals in some places and ask harder questions, and abilities like transformations can be used in encounters. Battles are planned below.

### Battles

Agreed on 18 October 2026, with mockups. A battle looks like an animal encounter and plays like a Pokémon battle, but every hit or miss depends on whether you know the word: no levels, no luck, no timers.

- **A round.** Your turn: buttons in Chinese with pinyin and English, 打 (dǎ, attack), 吃 (chī, eat a peach, when you're hurt and have one) and 跑 (pǎo, run away; not from big enemies). Your move asks a question: right, you hit; wrong, you miss. Then the enemy's turn: it says where it's attacking from or what it throws (草！ 石头！ 火！) and you pick what you heard: right, you dodge; wrong, it hits you, and you lose health (10 for each of the creatures on Flower Fruit Mountain).
- **Moves come from the story**, and harder ones ask harder questions and hit harder: 打 with your stick (from the prologue; a Listen question, hitting for 10), 跳 jump (the enemy's next attack misses), 变成 transform (Area 2: your next question loses a wrong answer), and the staff, 金箍棒 (Area 3: a Read question, hitting for 20; your stick becomes the staff).
- **No friend powers** (decided 18 October 2026, after playtesting): planned, then dropped to keep battles simple. Abilities like transforming (变成) come later, with the story.
- **Who you fight.** On Flower Fruit Mountain, wild creatures on the path up the stream: snakes and bees in the long grass, a wild boar blocking the last stretch, all chased away, never hurt. Demons at the end of Area 2 (little demons, then the Demon of Havoc, 混世魔王), the Dragon King's guards in Area 3, Heaven's soldiers in Area 4.
- **Where.** Wild creatures jump out of long grass on paths, drawn like the meadow's tall grass, a shade darker; bushes, boulders and banks mean you can't always go round it, and standing in it hides your feet, as in Pokémon. Which creature jumps out is chance, from those that live there, but never the same three times in a row, unless it's all that lives there (decided 22 October 2026: by chance, a spider jumped out six times in a row). The meadow's tall grass keeps its friendly animals. Big enemies (the boar, demons) stand on the map guarding the way.
- **Bosses** come in stages; between them the boss says a line and you answer by filling the gap in a sentence (你是谁？ … 我是美猴王！).
- **Winning and losing.** The first meeting with a kind of enemy teaches its name on a card. Winning gives shells or coins, sometimes an item. Losing isn't punished: you're carried back to rest till your health is full (in the meadow, or by a bed in the Water Curtain Cave once you've found it), and the words you missed come back sooner. Health comes back by eating a peach, resting with the old monkey, or sleeping (睡觉) in a bed in the Water Curtain Cave, which a monkey just inside the entrance tells you about once the troop has moved in (decided 19 October 2026).
- **Getting harder without levels.** The path's creatures ask about words you've known a while, with pictures; later enemies use harder kinds of question (characters by sound, sentence gaps, tones), and bosses mix them all.

**Combat in later areas** (weapons, moves, transformations, the gourd, armour and the bosses, area by area) is planned in the [combat plan](https://claude.ai/artifact/UrUFXQVvUzBjvr85wTeB7R), written 27 October 2026 and waiting to be agreed; once it is, it comes into this section.

**Health** (decided 27 October 2026; until then, hearts). You start with 50, and it carries over between battles. Only enemies cost health; animal encounters never do. Each enemy has its own health and hits for its own amount, and your weapon hits for its own (the stick 10), so later weapons, moves (跳, 变成) and enemies can differ by a little; eating a peach brings back 10 (it counts as using the word), and later food can bring back more. Resting refills it: in the prologue the old monkey, waiting at the foot of the path; from chapter 1 the cave's stone beds; somewhere to stay in each later area. The story raises the most you can have, above all the Peaches of Immortality in Heaven (Area 4), never fighting the same creatures over and over. It shows as a bar (Pokémon's way): a heart, then the bar, green, yellow under half, red at a fifth or less, sliding down as you're hit; on each name plate in a battle, yours with its number beside You (an enemy's without); at the top of the map while you're hurt; and in your bag. **A fight is set by how many right answers it takes, and the numbers are chosen to match**: about 2 to 5 hits for a wild creature, more for a boss (in stages), so the numbers never become the point. (Until 27 October 2026 you had 5 hearts and each hit took one: too few to tell weapons, food and enemies apart, and a boss's hearts wouldn't fit on its plate. Hearts stay for friendship: an animal's hearts fill as you answer.) Health first appears when the big monkey gives you the stick.

The box is kept small (decided 24 October 2026, from a mock-up): the speaker's name, then the line, its pinyin big, a little smaller than its characters, and as bright (for a beginner the sound matters more than the characters), a word's characters always together, however wide its pinyin, with a word's meaning beside it when you tap it; then its English, just as wide as its words, with ▼ beside it. Full cards are kept for new words, quizzes, the shop and the story. A line of story in a conversation (such as the old monkey sending you off to explore) waits in the speech box too, so the next line can't cover it, with ▼ beside it. What to do after a line (where to go, how to play) is a message of its own, straight after it, never under it (decided 24 October 2026: it crowded the line): its first sentence in bold, the rest under it, and an icon it starts with, such as the sparkle, big beside it. A card's buttons always stay in sight at its bottom, however long the card, and a card with more than it shows says so: ▼ More, by its buttons (left of them, where there's room), until you reach its end; tap it, or press down on the D-pad, to scroll (decided 22 October 2026: on a phone in Chrome the Tasks page and How to play were cut off at the bottom, with no sign of more).
- **Bigger objects, with today's graphics** (decided 17 October 2026, from mockups that also tried richer tiles and new characters): trees, the fruit trees, your stone, the rocks and the stall are drawn bigger than their squares, in today's colours (the practice post went back to its one-square look, which looked better). A block of forest two squares by two is one big round tree, other trees are taller, and the stall is three squares wide. What reaches up above an object's squares (leaves, the awning) is drawn over the characters, so you can walk behind things. Walking through a peach or apple tree's wide branches (the squares right beside it), it rustles once, gently, dropping a couple of leaves; walking behind trees, it doesn't. The ground, water, mountain and characters keep today's look.
- **Your tasks only list what you've found** (decided 17 October 2026): the chapter's checklist shows a quest once you've met it (the big monkey's flowers once he has asked), or once it's done, then "More to find as you play" while some are still hidden. Finding things is part of the fun, so the list never gives them away.
- **Start and Select** (decided 17 October 2026), like a Game Boy, under the D-pad: Start opens the menu and Select your tasks, both with your shells, words and friends at the top. They replace the header (title, stats and a Menu button) and the task box under the screen, so the screen can be taller. A new task is announced with a message, and a red dot stays on Select until you've looked. The title screen's words and the opening's buttons are in the picture. They are labelled TASKS and MENU (decided 18 October 2026), so it is clear what they do. Two more of the same, BAG and MAP, sit under A and B (decided 26 October 2026, where the speaker was): BAG opens your bag, and MAP will open a map of the area (for now, it says it's coming soon).
- **The menu** has six big tiles, each with an icon, Chinese and English: 词 Words (the Word Scroll), 任务 Tasks, 包 Bag, 朋友 Friends, 衣服 Clothes (the wardrobe) and 保存 Save; each tile's English is what its Chinese means. Tap a friend in 朋友 Friends, and its page opens, the same as its word's page in the Word Scroll: its picture, Chinese and pinyin, with its English and how it's made behind the English button (decided 24 October 2026: it only said its name; a monkey friend's name isn't a word, so it still just says it). A Settings page holds music, sound effects, pinyin, English on replies, frames a second (off to start with: a counter under the power light, on the console, so nothing on the screen covers it), How to play, the opening and the title screen. The game draws at most about 60 frames a second (decided 26 October 2026): its screen is so small that frames in between would look the same and only use up the battery, and everything moves by the clock, so it plays at the same speed on any screen. The game saves itself; Save says so ("Saved: Game 1, just now"), because players expect a Save button.
- **Three games (save slots)** (decided 18 October 2026), like a Game Boy game, so two people can share a phone. The title screen shows them side by side, each with its words, chapter and when it was last played, or empty. Choosing one opens its card: Continue, Copy to a file, or Delete (asked once more, with Keep it as the main button, and a copy kept in the browser); an empty one has New game or Load from a file. Each game keeps its own settings, and a new game starts with the sound and reading settings you're using. Your game from before is Game 1, unchanged. Saving, loading and starting again moved here from Settings.
- **The Word Scroll** is a grid of sprites, with shadows for words you haven't caught and category tabs across the top. Today's words is a filter in it.
- **Played sideways, like a Game Boy Advance** (decided 17 October 2026). There's one layout: the screen in the middle, always 15 columns by 10 rows (the GBA's shape, 3:2), as big as fits; the D-pad, Select and Start on the left with a power light; A and B on the right with a speaker. A tall screen on an upright phone looked strange, so held upright a card asks you to turn the phone, and the game waits (if the phone's rotation lock is on, it has to be switched off). Every place must be at least 15 by 10 (a test checks). Cards sit over the screen, so they only have its height: they use its width instead (pictures beside words, answers in a row), and text and pictures follow the screen's height (13 to 18px text; pinyin never under 10.5px, so its tone marks can be read), so the same layouts fit an iPhone, an Android phone and a computer. On a small phone (an iPhone, about 270px of screen, and smaller) cards and the speech box are a little tighter, so the main thing on each fits and you stay in sight. It can be installed as an app (manifest.webmanifest, sw.js) to get the whole screen: on Android it opens full screen and stays sideways; on an iPhone it opens without Safari's bars, but the phone must still be turned.

## Technical approach

The current game is one hand-built map in one file. Adding areas that way would become unmanageable, so the engine is split from the content first.

- **Data-driven areas.** Each area is a block of data: its map, words, characters, lines, quests, shop stock and exit test. The engine reads it, so adding an area is writing content, not new code.
- **A shared word bank.** Every word is defined once, with its pinyin, meaning, category, sprite and the area it belongs to. The scheduler, the Word Scroll, shops and encounters all read from it.
- **Pixel pictures for every collectible.** Emoji look different on every device and clash with the pixel style, so animals, food and items get their own pixel pictures. They're 32 × 32 (decided 17 October 2026: at 16 × 16 they looked too rough on cards, and 你好 (hello) was hard to read), drawn from shapes in tools/draw-pictures.js, which shades and outlines them, so a new one is quick to make. They're shown at sizes that give each pixel a whole number of the screen's real pixels, so they stay sharp. The animals walking on the map stay 16 × 16, a square's size. This is the biggest cost per area.
- **Saving.** Progress stays in the browser, plus a save export and import, so a cleared browser doesn't lose weeks of play.
- **Audio.** Voice clips made in advance (since 19 October 2026; before, the device's Chinese voice). The plan (19 October 2026): pick a computer voice from Azure with a listening test (tools/try-voices.html), then make an audio file for every word and line in advance, so every device sounds the same and works offline, with the device's voice for anything without a file. Recorded native audio replaces the files later, under the same names, once the game is finished. **Chosen after the listening test (19 October 2026): Yunxi (zh-CN-YunxiNeural, a young man) for the words and most lines, and Yunze (zh-CN-YunzeNeural, an older man) for the old monkey.** **Changed after playing it on a Pixel 8 Pro (19 October 2026):** slowed down by 15%, with silence and breaths around it, Yunxi sounded slow and exaggerated. The second listening test chose Yunxi at normal speed, tidy (no silence before or after, short pauses between sentences) and in its relaxed chat style for sentences, and showed that giving a voice the pinyin fixes a word it reads wrongly (只). A native speaker agreed that single words sound clearer in a woman's voice, so words said on their own (new-word cards, tapping a word, listening questions) get their own voice: Xiaoxiao (zh-CN-XiaoxiaoNeural), chosen in a third test. In the game some single syllables (水, 草, 喝) still sounded strange in every Xiaoxiao setting, while the Pixel's own (Google's) voice said them well, so a fifth test chose Google's Standard A (cmn-CN-Standard-A) for words (20 October 2026); its clips are made on GitHub, which keeps Google's key. Lines keep Yunxi, and the old monkey Yunze, tidied the same way.
- **Drawing the map.** A place's map is drawn once when you go there (its ground, and the big objects that never move, into layers under and over the characters); when the story changes a few squares (a dead pine knocked down), only they are drawn again, with what's around them (decided 22 October 2026: drawing the whole pine wood again took two seconds on a phone). Each frame then just copies the part you see.
- **Format.** A web game that runs in a phone browser, hosted on GitHub Pages and built in Claude Code.

## Risks and open questions

| Risk | What could go wrong | Plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | A full RPG is a lot to build | Data-driven areas, built a chapter at a time; finish Area 1 well before adding Area 2 |
| A long first area | Area 1 has two chapters, about 50 words | Chapter 1 mostly reuses the prologue's words, and new places keep it fresh; playtest the prologue first |
| Art | Every collectible needs a sprite | Reuse palettes and shapes; aim for 15–20 new sprites per area |
| Time per session | Walking and menus crowd out learning | Target at least half of play time hearing, reading or answering |
| Reward farming | Players repeat easy words for rewards | Shells only for words that need practice, as above |
| Chinese accuracy | Unnatural lines as grammar gets harder | Native-speaker review of each area's lines |
| Lost progress | A cleared browser wipes the save | Save export and import |

**Decided** (these were the open questions)

- **Words per chapter:** no fixed number since 20 October 2026 (Flower Fruit Mountain's prologue has 45, and its chapter 1 about 40). It was about 25 new words to collect, like the prologue (27: 28 once the shop added 老板 and 头带 and 森林 was dropped, then 路 moved to chapter 1), so about 20 new sprites per chapter. (Area 1 has two chapters, decided 17 October 2026.) Words you only hear in sentences don't count towards this.
- **Demons:** harder battles start at the end of Area 2, as in the story: Sun Wukong comes home from his teacher and defeats the Demon of Havoc (混世魔王), who has been bullying the monkeys.
- **Pinyin** stays over every word (the menu setting turns it off); it doesn't fade as you learn words.
- **After the headband:** the 老板 also sells 苹果, bought with 我要苹果; eating one in an animal encounter takes away a wrong answer.
- **Native speaker:** someone can check the game's Chinese, from a review sheet listing every line with pinyin and English.
- **Grammar:** the order in the table of sentence patterns stays as it is. Patterns can be heard in earlier areas before an area uses them a lot.

**Decided on 16 October 2026** (from the [second review](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/a02dc745-404f-4605-9b78-00d70691112e))

- **Phrases** are their own collection, outside the words counted for each area.
- **我不要** is taught in Area 1 as a fixed phrase; 不 on its own waits for Area 2.
- **Talk** moves into a speech box over the map; full cards only for the big moments.
- **The wardrobe** starts in Area 1 with the headband, and opens fully in Area 2. (A flower behind your ear was tried and taken out.)
- **The map** stays compact and gets livelier now; it splits into small places with the multi-map work. (Later the same day: it grew to the south, 24 × 22 tiles, to give the squirrel and goat their homes, and the 老板's market stall and the little monkey moved down there so the top isn't crowded.)
- **Day and night:** a day ends with sleep in the cave and a summary. (Tried, then taken out on 16 October 2026: see below.)
- **Native speaker check:** 再见, 不客气, 对不起, 没关系, 我不要, 早上好, 晚安, 我戴头带, 我穿衣服 and 这是山 are all fine as written.

**Decided on 16 October 2026: no days or sleeping.** A nest, a day count, an end-of-day summary and good night and good morning scenes were built and then taken out: people play at any time and for any length, so a day rhythm gave no real reason to sleep. Reviews followed real time (1, 3, 7 and 21 days) until 20 October 2026; now they're a list worked through in turns (see The review list). 早上好 and 晚安 left the phrases with it; they're checked, and can come back if a good place for them turns up. The book of firsts was taken out too. The repetition target above (8 times in a word's first week) assumed daily play and needs rethinking with the scheduler.

**Decided on 16 October 2026: a vocabulary game, never a grammar test.** Sentence tasks always give the whole sentence in Chinese and English with one word missing, and you choose the word; word tiles in order are gone. The choices follow the word's level (pictures while new, then Chinese and pinyin). This makes the game more relaxed while still giving lots of repeated input of simple sentence structures.

**Decided after playtesting stage 2** (16 October 2026): Flower Fruit Mountain must suit someone who speaks no Chinese yet, so they can spend longer there getting to know its words.

- **No emoji** (decided 18 October 2026): every icon is pixel art like the rest of the game (a star, a scroll, a clipboard, a paw, a shirt, a cog, a floppy disk, a book, a cup, mountains, the shell, the speaker and the heart), drawn in tools/draw-pictures.js at 16 × 16. The text still says them as emoji, and the game shows the pixel icon instead.
- **Writing in the world is real Chinese** (decided 21 October 2026, when the school's sign had made-up squiggles on it): a sign, a tablet or a blackboard says something real, in characters drawn by hand in pixels, so there's always more to read: 学校 on the school's sign, 水帘洞 carved down the cave's stone tablet, the numbers 一 to 十 on the blackboard. Words from the word bank where it can be, or a name. Word pictures (a question's answers) have no writing in them: a word written in one could give an answer away (三 on the teacher's blackboard, in a question about 三).
- **The question layout** (decided 18 October 2026): a question starts at the top, with the speaker beside "Listen. Which picture is it?", and a bottom row whose space is always kept: what happened ("Yes! 山 shān") on the left and Next on the right, so nothing moves when you answer. When listening, the answers show their characters, and their pinyin once you've answered. Food from the shop is a small hint in that bottom row ("Eat a 苹果 (apple): one wrong answer goes"), in plain text, not a big button. The pixel font is kept for short English labels and buttons; Chinese and sentences are never in it.
- **Questions get harder slowly:** two right answers in a row at each step, and no picking characters by sound (without pictures) on Flower Fruit Mountain.
- **Building a sentence** marks its words as used, but doesn't skip the questions: a word is Used once you can read it too.
- **Every word you practise has a picture,** including 瀑布, 老板 and 头带. 我 and 要 are practised by building 我要 sentences at the practice post.
- **The story at the waterfall** is a short one made of sentences you've already met, each with a picture, and its questions fill a gap with a picture. The longer story from the book's opening moves to the Water Curtain Cave, which teaches 有, 一只 and 一个.
